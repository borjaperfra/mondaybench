PLATFORM PROGRAMME - CONSOLIDATED DOSSIER

This dossier is the full set of material that has accumulated around the
platform work: runbook extracts, rosters, decision logs, correspondence, chat
excerpts, risk register entries and background reports. It has been assembled
from several sources and is deliberately not deduplicated, because the group
wants the provenance of each statement to remain visible.

All times are on a 24-hour clock. Everything operational described here happens
on the same day unless a date is given.

===============================================================================
SECTION 1 - PROGRAMME HISTORY
===============================================================================

The platform group entered the year with three programmes on its plate. The
first, the reporting warehouse consolidation, completed in February, five weeks
ahead of its forecast, largely because the vendor's migration tool turned out to
handle nested partitions without the manual intervention the estimate had
assumed. The second, the identity broker refresh, was deferred to next year
after the security review asked for a threat model that nobody had budgeted time
to write. The third is the subject of this dossier.

Teodor produced the first scoping document in April. It ran to nine pages and
proposed a three-day exercise with an intermediate state held between days two
and three. Ines revised it twice. Her first revision followed the capacity
review and trimmed two pages of contingency that the review had shown to be
unnecessary. Her second followed the vendor call of 22 April, at which the
vendor confirmed that the intermediate state could be skipped entirely, and that
revision is what collapsed the exercise into a single day.

Marek took over the runbook in May. He has asked repeatedly that changes go
through him rather than being edited in place. This is not bureaucracy: the
June incident on the notification relay was traced to two engineers editing the
same runbook section within the same hour, each unaware of the other, producing
a procedure that referenced a step that no longer existed. The post-incident
review recommended a single owner per runbook and that recommendation was
adopted across the group.

Anselm, who joined in June, has been reading the dossier as onboarding material
and has filed four clarification tickets against it. Three were about wording.
The fourth pointed out that an early draft had two different figures for the
same measurement, which was true, and the draft was corrected. None of these
tickets changed the plan.

===============================================================================

provisioned headroom on a normal weekday, rising to 68% during the month-end

plan and neither should be used in any calculation.

Sofia's cost note puts the incremental infrastructure cost of the day at roughly
2,400 units, most of it temporary storage. Finance approved it on 11 July. The
approval expires at the end of the quarter, which is after the day and is
therefore not a constraint.

The event bus carries about 18,000 messages a minute at peak and is untouched by
this work. The billing reconciler is untouched. The media transcoder fleet was
resized in June and is irrelevant here. The search tier is in the middle of its
own migration, which Yusuf and Kiran are covering, and which shares no equipment

SECTION 21 - HOW THE WORK IS PERFORMED
===============================================================================

The mechanics below are group convention and apply to every step without
exception.

One person performs one task at a time, no task may be paused once it has started, and a task must fit entirely inside the availability window of the person performing it.

The convention about pausing comes from the June incident: a step that was
paused and resumed left a lock behind and the lock was what took the notification
relay down. The group has not allowed a paused step since.
