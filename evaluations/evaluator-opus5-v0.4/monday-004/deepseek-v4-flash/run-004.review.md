## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Reduce incremental investment in the Mid-market segment.**  
   - Mid-market active MRR declined 10.2% in one month.  
   - Nakatomi churned and Soylent downgraded and is generating critical support tickets.  
   - Do not cut support for existing Mid-market customers — especially Soylent — but pause new Mid-market acquisition spend until retention and account health improve.

2. **Increase spend on the Partner channel, but only after validating cost data.**  
   - Partner has the highest trial conversion rate (~23–25%) and the highest average new-logo ACV (€83.4k).  
   - However, October shows no partner customer conversions yet in acquisition.csv, and CAC payback is unknown.  
   - Recommendation: run a small, controlled increase in partner spend and track trial-to-paid conversion and CAC before scaling.

3. **Focus engineering on the Pro plan.**  
   - Pro plan has the best margin but is showing strain: rate-limit tickets more than doubled, and more customers are exceeding included requests.  
   - Address capacity/limits, especially for Wonka, Aperture, and Vandelay.  
   - Also consider whether Pro pricing should be adjusted for overage, since usage is growing.

4. **Investigate Enterprise plan cost creep.**  
   - Enterprise revenue was flat, but infra and support costs rose ~€18k in October.  
   - Soylent’s critical issues and the general high volume of high-severity tickets suggest support cost may be reactive.  
   - This is a cost-efficiency issue, not necessarily a reason to reduce investment in Enterprise customers.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

- **CAC payback cannot be calculated** — no acquisition spend data is provided.
- **Funnel conversions are paid-trial starts, not paid customers.** They cannot be directly linked to acquisition.csv converted customers.
- **October revenue.csv includes a €120k annual prepay from Gringotts** and a **duplicate Soylent transaction** (same transaction ID from Stripe and billing_sync). I used plans.csv for MRR and excluded one-time/PS revenue.
- **Plans.csv October customer counts include churned customers** (e.g., Nakatomi and 4 SMB starters). Active MRR had to be adjusted by removing churned customers’ revenue.
- **Segment-level costs are not directly available.** Plan-level costs were allocated to segments using customer counts, which assumes equal cost per customer. In reality, Enterprise-segment customers use far more requests than Mid-market enterprise-plan customers, so cost allocation is approximate.
- **Expansion bookings are not yet in MRR** — they take effect in the next billing cycle.
- **Acme’s request drop is a workload change, not churn.** Token volume fell only 12% vs 45% request volume, so the revenue/usage impact is unclear.
- **Small sample sizes** for acquisition channel analysis (especially partner: only 2 new logos in the period) make conclusions directional, not definitive.
