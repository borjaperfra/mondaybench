## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

### 1. Customer segment investment

**Do not reduce Enterprise investment.** It is flat, high-ARR, and the source of most absolute contribution.

**The weakest segment on available data is SMB:**
- Lowest ARR per customer: **€34.2k** vs Mid-market €170k and Enterprise €847.5k.
- Highest logo churn: **4 of 13 SMB customers churned in October**.
- Only **€1.5k MRR per support ticket** in October, vs €22.6k for Enterprise.

Reallocate or reduce SMB acquisition/support investment until unit economics improve. However, no segment-level spend data is provided, so validate before cutting.

**Mid-market should not be cut; it needs retention focus**, especially on Soylent.

### 2. Acquisition channel spend

**Do not increase spend yet.** The data cannot support a CAC payback decision.

If a controlled test is required, **paid search is the best candidate** because it drove the most attributed revenue and the only high-value expansions. But monitor marginal trial conversion, which is declining.

### 3. Product / engineering focus

**Focus on the Enterprise plan.**

It is the weakest after cost and the largest absolute contribution at risk. A 10% reduction in Enterprise infra + support costs would add roughly **€46.5k/month** — more than Pro’s entire monthly contribution.

Address **Pro rate limits** as a secondary priority to protect the highest-margin growth plan.

### 4. CFO request

**CAC payback by acquisition channel cannot be calculated from this pack.** Add:
- Marketing spend by channel.
- Trial-to-paid conversion tracking.
- Channel-level contribution margin.

- **No marketing spend data**: CAC payback cannot be calculated.
- **Funnel data tracks trial starts, not paid customers**: trial-to-paid conversion is not tracked.
- **Costs are available by plan, not by customer segment**: segment-level contribution is approximate.
- **Usage account mapping matters**: Wayne Enterprises uses `wayne_ent`; Prestige Worldwide uses `prestige_ww`.
- **Acme’s usage decline is expected** from the batch migration and should not be treated as churn or product failure.
