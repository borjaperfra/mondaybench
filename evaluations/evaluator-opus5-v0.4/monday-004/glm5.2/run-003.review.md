# Northstar — Monday Business Review

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

- **SMB Starter is the weakest segment by every available lens**: 4 of ~14 Starter customers churned in October (≈29% of Starter logos, ≈26% of Starter ARR), support cost is ~32% of plan revenue, and absolute contribution margin is the lowest of any plan.
- **October headline revenue is inflated by a one-off**: Gringotts moved from monthly to an annual invoice (€120k booked in October vs €10k/month). Underlying MRR growth is modest.
- **Pro is the strongest plan on contribution margin (~63–67%)** and has near-zero churn; it deserves product attention despite being smaller than Enterprise.
- **Paid search volume scaled sharply (leads +73%) but trial conversion rate fell from 8.5% to 6.4%**, even though paid search sourced three large Enterprise expansions (Stark, Umbrella, Globex = €174k ACV). The signal is mixed and CAC payback cannot be calculated from this pack.
- **Acme's 45% request drop is explained** by the late-September batch migration; token volume only fell ~12%, so this is not a health problem.
- **Soylent is a mid-market at-risk account**: usage −27%, a *critical* ticket in October, and a cluster of high-severity api_errors/performance tickets.

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

### Decision 1 — Reduce investment in a customer segment: **Yes, reduce investment in SMB Starter.**
- Highest churn (4 logos, ~20% of segment ARR in one month).
- Weakest unit economics after support cost (~32% margin, support ≈ 32% of revenue).
- Low utilisation (avg ~84k of 200k included requests) — customers are not extracting value.
- All four October churns had preceding support tickets; the segment is consuming disproportionate support load for low return.
- **Action**: stop actively acquiring Starter logos; migrate viable Starter customers up to Pro; tighten support SLA on Starter to reduce loaded cost; revisit pricing/packaging before re-investing.

### Decision 2 — Increase spend on an acquisition channel: **Conditional — do not blanket-increase paid search yet.**
- Paid search lead volume already grew 73% and trial conversion fell from 8.5% to 6.4% — diminishing returns are visible.
- However, paid search sourced €174k of Enterprise expansion ACV in October, which is high-value.
- **Action**: increase paid search spend *only on the Enterprise-expansion cohort* (lookalike/ABM-style), not the broad SMB funnel. Require channel spend data before any further increase. Partner's 23% trial conversion warrants a small, test-budget increase given its quality, but volume scalability is unproven.

### Decision 3 — Product plan focus: **Pro.**
- Best contribution margin (~63–67%), zero churn, low utilisation headroom (room to add features/usage without infra step-cost), and it sits between the churning Starter tier and the high-support Enterprise tier — a natural upgrade path for at-risk Starter customers and a lower-friction entry for Mid-market.
- **Secondary**: investigate Enterprise support-cost inflation (api_errors/performance tickets are recurring across Stark, Wayne, Contoso, Cyberdyne, Nakatomi, Soylent). A reliability/observability investment would protect €6.8M of ARR and likely reduce the €168k/month support load.

### Additional actions not on the agenda but warranted
- **Soylent**: executive sponsor engagement immediately — critical ticket, −27% usage, cluster of high-severity issues. This is the next likely Mid-market churn.
- **Duplicate invoice**: reverse the Soylent `billing_sync` duplicate before October books close.
- **Nakatomi post-mortem**: the September high-severity api_errors/performance tickets were a leading indicator of the October churn. Build a churn-risk score from ticket severity patterns.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

1. **CAC payback by channel is not calculable.** No marketing spend by channel is provided. The CFO's request cannot be answered from this pack; Finance/Growth must supply spend data.
2. **Duplicate invoice for Soylent** (INV-2026-10-0209 in both `stripe` and `billing_sync`). October subscription revenue is overstated by €19,500 until reversed.
3. **Gringotts annual invoice distorts October headline revenue.** €120k booked in October vs €10k/month otherwise; any MoM revenue comparison must use monthly-equivalent MRR, not invoiced revenue.
4. **Trial-to-paid conversion is not tracked** in this pack, so funnel conversion rates only cover leads → paid trials, not leads → customers. Channel ROI claims based on `attributed_revenue_eur` rely on marketing-platform attribution that cannot be validated here.
5. **Single-touch attribution**: each converted lead has exactly one channel. Multi-touch influence is invisible; the three Enterprise expansions "attributed" to paid search may have been nurtured by other channels.
6. **One month of churn data is not a trend.** Four Starter churns in October is alarming but could be coincidental (e.g., a cohort that started together reaching a renewal cliff). Need 2–3 more months before treating 20% as the steady-state Starter churn rate.
7. **Support cost is fully loaded** (includes CS staff), but we cannot split reactive vs. proactive cost, so we cannot quantify how much Starter support is "wasted" vs. value-adding.
8. **No cohort/tenure data on churned accounts beyond start_date.** The four churned Starter accounts started 2024-09 to 2025-10 — a wide range — so this is not obviously a single bad cohort.
9. **`usage_change_pct` is missing for new October customers** (spacely, cogswell, bishop, gekko, paper-street) — expected, but it means we cannot assess new-logo activation quality yet.
10. **Acme's request drop is explained but token drop (−12%) is not fully reconciled** with the "40–50% interactive reduction" estimate. Worth confirming the batch migration is performing as planned, but not a red flag.
