# Northstar — Monday Business Review

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

- **Headline October revenue looks strong but is misleading.** The apparent jump is driven by (a) Gringotts switching from monthly to annual billing (€120k invoiced upfront, but ARR unchanged at €120k), (b) a duplicate Soylent invoice (€19.5k, `billing_sync` vs `stripe`), and (c) churned customers still being billed on Oct 1 before churning mid-month.
- **Underlying MRR is actually declining.** Net new + expansion MRR (~€11.6k/mo) is more than offset by churn (~€25.3k/mo). October forward MRR ≈ €733k vs September €747k, a ~1.8% decline.
- **Churn is concentrated in SMB Starter.** Four of five October churns were Starter-plan SMB customers (slate-rock, mega-lo-mart, dinoco, buy-n-large). The Starter plan acquired 4 new customers in October and lost 4 — net zero growth despite acquisition spend.
- **Enterprise remains the economic engine** (≈79% of gross profit) but its margin is eroding: infra + support costs rose ~€18k/mo while revenue was essentially flat.
- **Paid Search scaled aggressively** (leads +73%) but trial conversion rate fell (8.5% → 6.4%). It did, however, drive three large enterprise expansions in October (Stark, Umbrella, Globex = €174k attributed).
- **CAC payback by channel cannot be calculated** — the pack contains no channel spend data.

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

### Decision 1: Reduce investment in one customer segment — **Yes, reduce SMB Starter investment.**

- 4 of 5 October churns were Starter SMB. The plan acquired 4 and lost 4 in the same month — net zero.
- GP per Starter customer is €792/year; the plan contributes only ~4% of total GP.
- Starter support cost (€10.3k/mo for 13 customers) is disproportionate to revenue (€32k).
- **Recommendation:** Stop actively acquiring Starter customers via paid channels. Keep the plan as a low-touch self-serve entry point, but redirect acquisition spend and CS capacity toward Pro and Enterprise. Consider a price increase or usage-based pricing for Starter to filter for higher-intent customers.

### Decision 2: Increase spend on one acquisition channel — **Conditionally, increase Content; hold Paid Search until attribution is fixed.**

- **Content** has the second-highest trial conversion rate (~10–12%), consistent new-logo output (3 logos in 2 months), and lower risk of attribution distortion since it drives net-new names.
- **Paid Search** drove significant *attributed* expansion revenue (€210k), but those expansions are on long-tenured enterprise customers (Stark since 2023, Umbrella since 2024, Globex since 2024) "converting" within days of a campaign start. This is almost certainly last-touch attribution noise, not causal acquisition. Scaling paid_search further also showed deteriorating conversion rates (8.5% → 6.4%).
- **Partner** has the best conversion rate but too little volume to scale meaningfully based on this data.
- **Recommendation:** Increase Content spend modestly. Do not increase Paid Search spend until (a) channel spend data is available to compute CAC payback, and (b) expansion attribution is validated. If leadership still wants to scale Paid Search, run a holdout test on existing customers to isolate causal effect.

### Decision 3: Which plan deserves engineering focus — **Enterprise, for cost reduction; Pro, for growth enablement.**

- **Enterprise** generates 79% of GP but margins are eroding (−2.6pp in one month) and infra/support costs are rising with no revenue growth. Engineering focus should target infra cost efficiency (the batch-migration pattern Acme adopted could be productised for other enterprise customers) and support load reduction (enterprise accounts generate disproportionate high-severity tickets — api_errors and performance dominate).
- **Pro** is the best-margin plan and the natural upgrade path for healthy Starter customers. Engineering should ensure Pro can scale to handle customers approaching their included-request limits (Wonka, Vandelay, Aperture are all hitting rate_limits).
- **Starter should not receive significant engineering investment.** The economics don't justify it.

### CFO request: CAC payback by channel
- **Cannot be delivered from this pack.** No marketing spend data is provided. Recommend Finance and Growth jointly produce a channel-level spend file (by month, by channel) so CAC = spend ÷ new-logo ACV and payback = CAC ÷ GP-per-month can be computed.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

1. **Duplicate invoice.** Soylent's October subscription appears twice (INV-2026-10-0209 in both `stripe` and `billing_sync`, €19.5k each). One must be removed before any revenue reporting. This inflates October headline revenue by €19.5k.

2. **Annual invoice distorts monthly revenue comparison.** Gringotts' €120k annual invoice makes October cash revenue appear €110k higher than September with no underlying ARR change. Monthly recurring revenue (MRR) should be the primary revenue metric, not invoiced cash.

3. **CAC payback is not calculable.** The acquisition file has attributed revenue but no spend. The funnel file has leads and conversions but no cost per lead. The CFO's request cannot be answered.

4. **Expansion attribution is unreliable.** Three enterprise customers with 2–3 year tenure (Stark, Umbrella, Globex) are attributed to paid_search campaigns that started 2026-09-28 and "converted" within 2–4 weeks. This pattern is inconsistent with genuine new-customer acquisition and likely reflects last-touch attribution of organic/CS-led expansions. Any channel ROI conclusion based on attributed expansion revenue should be treated as directional, not definitive.

5. **Trial-to-paid conversion is not tracked.** The funnel only covers leads → paid trials. We cannot compute true customer acquisition conversion rates or compare channels end-to-end.

6. **Plan customer counts include churned customers in their churn month.** October Starter shows 13 customers, but 4 churned mid-month. Active Starter customers at month-end = 9, same as September. Plan-level economics for October overstate the active base.

7. **Soylent revenue change is unexplained.** September invoice was €22k; October is €19.5k; current ARR is €234k (= €19.5k/mo). The September figure may reflect a prior rate, a credit, or a billing error. Needs clarification before drawing conclusions about Soylent's contract status.

8. **No cohort or tenure data for churn analysis.** We cannot determine whether Starter churn is concentrated in specific vintages or uniform. All four churned Starter customers had 12–18 months tenure, but the sample is too small to generalise.

9. **Support cost allocation method is unclear.** The data dictionary says support_cost_eur includes "fully loaded cost of customer-success staff assigned to [the plan]," but we cannot verify how staff time is allocated across plans or whether it reflects actuals or budgeted headcount.

10. **Usage_change_pct for new October customers is blank** (spacely, cogswell, bishop, gekko, paper-street), as expected — but this means we cannot assess early usage trajectory for newly acquired accounts.
