## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Three headline findings shape this morning's decisions:**

1. **SMB is churning at an alarming rate.** 4 of 18 SMB customers (22%) churned in October, all on the Starter plan, representing €10,300/month in lost MRR. Each showed a 34–37% usage decline in the weeks before churning. The Starter plan generates only €792 in monthly margin per customer and is effectively a revolving door.

2. **Headline MRR growth is illusory.** Plan-level MRR rose from €746,100 (Sep) to €757,700 (Oct), appearing to grow 1.6%. But October's figure includes €25,300/month from 5 customers who churned mid-month (still invoiced Oct 1). Post-churn, current MRR is **€732,400 — a 1.8% decline** from September.

3. **Paid_search attribution is contaminated.** The channel is credited with €238,800 in October "acquisition" revenue, but €174,000 (73%) comes from expansion deals on existing Enterprise customers (Stark, Umbrella, Globex) who have been with Northstar for 2–3 years. The credited campaign started September 28 — two days before October. This is last-touch attribution noise, not evidence of paid_search driving new business.

**Decisions supported by the data:**
- ✅ Reduce investment in SMB (specifically the Starter plan)
- ⚠️ Do not increase paid_search spend without first resolving the attribution problem; content is the better candidate but CAC cannot be calculated
- ✅ Direct engineering focus to the Enterprise plan (87% of revenue, key renewal this month, usage-limit and reliability issues)
- ❌ CAC payback by channel cannot be calculated — no spend data is provided

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

### Decision 1: Reduce investment in SMB — ✅ Supported

**Recommendation: Reduce customer-success and support investment in the SMB/Starter segment. Do not stop acquisition, but reallocate CS capacity.**

Rationale:
- 22% monthly churn (4 of 18 customers); 43% of Starter MRR churned in October.
- €792 monthly margin per customer — the thinnest of any plan.
- Support costs are 32% of revenue and growing 41% MoM.
- All 4 churned customers had 34–37% usage drops before churning — a leading indicator the team should monitor but that does not justify continued high-touch investment per customer.
- New SMB customers keep joining (4 in October), so the segment isn't dying — but the economics per customer don't support heavy investment.

**What to do:**
- Move Starter-plan support to lower-cost tiers (self-service, documentation, community).
- Implement a usage-decline alert (if requests drop >30% MoM, flag for automated re-engagement, not manual CS outreach).
- Do not reduce acquisition for SMB yet — the funnel is still producing new logos — but set a churn-rate threshold that triggers a pricing or packaging review.

### Decision 2: Increase spend on an acquisition channel — ⚠️ Conditional

**Recommendation: Do not increase paid_search spend. If any channel gets increased budget, it should be content, but only after spend data is provided to calculate CAC.**

Rationale:
- paid_search trial conversion rate is declining (8.5% → 6.4%) as volume scales — diminishing returns.
- paid_search's October attributed revenue (€238,800) is inflated by €174,000 of suspect expansion attributions on existing Enterprise customers. Real new-logo value is €64,800.
- Content has the best per-lead efficiency among new-logo channels (€138/lead vs €72 for paid_search), consistent trial conversion (10–12%), and produced 3 new logos in 2 months.
- Partner has the best conversion rate (22–25%) and brought the highest-value new logo (vandelay: €132K), but volume is very low (60–75 leads/month) and no October conversions appear in the acquisition data. Investigate whether partner can scale before increasing spend.

**What to do:**
- Finance must provide channel-level spend data before any spend increase is approved. The CFO's CAC payback question cannot be answered without it.
- Audit the attribution model: expansion bookings for existing customers should not be credited to a 2-day-old paid_search campaign.
- If spend data becomes available, calculate CAC by channel = total spend / new logos, and CAC payback = CAC / (monthly margin per customer).

### Decision 3: Product plan for engineering focus — ✅ Enterprise

**Recommendation: Focus next quarter's engineering on the Enterprise plan, with specific attention to overage handling, batch-processing support, and API reliability.**

Rationale:
- Enterprise generates 87% of revenue and has 0% churn — protecting it is the highest-leverage engineering investment.
- **Wayne Enterprises renews November 30** and is 14% over the 3M included request limit. Their usage is growing 10%/month. If overage billing isn't smooth, this renewal is at risk. (Wayne's ARR: €1,008,000.)
- Acme's shift to batch processing (−45% requests, +60% tokens/request) is a usage-pattern change that other Enterprise customers may follow. The platform needs to handle high-token, low-request batch workloads efficiently.
- Multiple Enterprise customers have open high-severity API error and performance tickets (Stark, Contoso, Wayne, Cyberdyne, Soylent).
- Soylent (Enterprise plan, Mid-market) downgraded from €22K to €19.5K MRR, had 8 support tickets in October (up from 3), and usage dropped 27%. This is a within-plan contraction that engineering can help prevent if API reliability improves.

**Secondary focus: Pro plan overage and upgrade path.**
- 3 of 9 Pro customers are over the 600K included limit (Wonka at 170%, Aperture at 118%, Vandelay at 115%). These are strong upsell candidates to Enterprise, but the overage experience needs to be good or they may churn instead of upgrading.
- Nakatomi (Pro) churned with an open high-severity API error ticket — same reliability theme.

**Do not direct engineering to Starter.** The Starter plan's problem is product-market fit and pricing (42% utilization, 43% MRR churn), not missing features. That's a product/commercial decision, not an engineering one.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

### Data quality problems

1. **Duplicate invoice — soylent, October.** Transaction `INV-2026-10-0209` appears twice in revenue.csv:
   - `2026-10-01, stripe, €19,500`
   - `2026-10-02, billing_sync, €19,500`
   
   Same transaction_id, different sources and dates. If both are summed, October revenue is overstated by €19,500. This appears to be a dual-load from two systems (Stripe + billing sync) rather than two genuine invoices. **The billing_sync record should be deduplicated before any revenue reporting.**

2. **Gringotts annual invoice recorded at full value.** The €120,000 annual subscription is recorded as a single October invoice in revenue.csv. For monthly revenue comparison, this should be amortized to €10,000/month. The plans.csv data correctly shows €10,000/month MRR, but anyone querying revenue.csv directly will overstate October by €110,000.

3. **Churned customers in October MRR.** Five customers churned mid-October but were invoiced Oct 1. October plan MRR (€757,700) includes €25,300 from these customers. Any report using plan MRR without adjusting for mid-month churn will overstate current revenue by €25,300.

### Unsupported causal claims

4. **Paid_search → Enterprise expansion attribution is not credible.** The acquisition table credits paid_search for €174,000 of expansion revenue from Stark (customer since 2023), Umbrella (since 2024), and Globex (since 2024). All three campaigns started September 28, 2026. There is no plausible mechanism by which a paid_search ad caused a 3-year-old Enterprise customer to expand. This is last-touch attribution noise. **Any decision to increase paid_search spend based on this attributed revenue would be unsupported.**

5. **Hooli expansion attribution (September, paid_search).** Hooli has been a customer since March 2024. The September expansion (€36,000 annualized) is attributed to a paid_search campaign starting September 7. Same issue as above — last-touch attribution for an existing customer.

6. **"Acme's usage decline is a problem."** Acme's −45% request volume is explicitly explained by the operational note (batch processing migration, 40–50% reduction expected). The actual −45% is within the forecast range. This is not a churn risk or a product failure — it is a planned usage pattern change. However, the tokens-per-request increase (+60%) and the downstream overage revenue reduction at renewal (Feb 2027) are legitimate forward concerns.

7. **"Partner channel doesn't work in October."** Partner had 17 trials in October but 0 conversions in acquisition.csv. This does not necessarily mean partner failed — trial-to-paid conversion "runs on a longer cycle and is not tracked in this pack." We simply cannot tell yet.

### Metrics that cannot be calculated reliably

8. **CAC by channel.** No spend data is provided. We have leads, trials, and attributed revenue, but not cost per lead, cost per trial, or total channel spend. **The CFO's CAC payback request cannot be fulfilled with this data pack.** Required: monthly spend per channel (at minimum, total spend and number of new logos per channel per month).

9. **Trial-to-paid conversion rate.** Explicitly stated as not tracked in this pack. The funnel shows leads → paid trials, but the subsequent conversion to paid customer is not available. We cannot calculate the full funnel conversion rate.

10. **Net Revenue Retention (NRR).** Can be approximated but not precisely calculated because:
    - We don't know the exact timing of when expansion/contraction takes effect vs. when it was booked.
    - Hooli's expansion was booked September 24 but took effect in October (per the data note: "Expansion bookings take effect from the following billing cycle").
    - Soylent's contraction (€22K → €19.5K) is visible in October invoices but the trigger date is unknown.
    - Approximate NRR (Sep → current): (746,100 − 25,300 churn − 2,500 contraction + 3,000 expansion) / 746,100 = **96.7%** — but this excludes the €21K/month of future expansions booked in October (Stark, Umbrella, Globex, Massive, Cyberdyne) that haven't taken effect yet.

11. **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) by plan.** We have 2 months of data. Churn rates based on 1 month are unreliable for LTV calculation. The Starter plan's 43% monthly MRR churn would imply a ~2.3-month lifetime, but this is based on a single month and includes customers who had been active for 12–25 months before churning.

12. **Overage revenue.** The plans note states "usage beyond [the included limit] is billed separately at renewal." We can identify customers over the limit (2 Enterprise, 3 Pro in October), but the overage rate and billing amount are not in the data. We cannot calculate the revenue impact of overage or the revenue risk from Acme's usage decline.
