## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**October MRR grew 1.6%, but the business is weaker than the headline suggests.**

- **MRR:** €757,700 in October, up €11,600 from September's €746,100.
- **Gross profit fell, not rose.** Total gross profit dropped from €262,900 to €246,500 (−6.2%) because costs increased faster than revenue. Enterprise plan costs rose €17,800 while its revenue was essentially flat.
- **Five customers churned in October**, all but one in the SMB starter tier, representing €303,600 in lost ARR against only €94,800 in new-logo ARR and €36,000 in expansion ARR recognized.
- **Projected November MRR ≈ €748,400**, below September's level, once churned customers drop off and October expansion bookings (+€16K/mo) partially offset the losses.
- **Soylent (mid-market, €234K ARR)** is an at-risk account: usage fell 27%, support tickets tripled to 8 including one critical-severity incident, and an open high-severity API error persists.
- **Wayne Enterprises (€1,008K ARR)** renews November 30 — 27 days from now — with an open high-severity performance ticket.

**Three decisions the data supports or constrains:**

| Decision | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Reduce investment in SMB segment | **Supported.** SMB generates 5.3% of MRR, drives a disproportionate share of churn and support load, and has thin margins. |
| Increase spend on one acquisition channel | **Cannot be decided from this data.** Channel spend is not provided, so no ROI or CAC payback can be calculated. Paid search has the most volume and attributed revenue, but we do not know its cost. |
| Which plan gets engineering focus | **Pro plan has the strongest business case.** It has the best margins (63%), highest utilization (74%), and clearest upgrade path. Enterprise cost optimisation is a close second priority. |

## 3. ACQUISITION

### 3.1 What the funnel and acquisition data show

The funnel tracks leads → paid trial (same month). The acquisition table tracks trial → paid customer (longer cycle, not tracked in funnel). These measure different stages.

| Channel | Sep Leads | Sep Trial Rate | Oct Leads | Oct Trial Rate | Oct Attributed Rev |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paid Search | 520 | 8.5% | 900 | 6.4% | €238,800 |
| Content | 260 | 11.9% | 330 | 10.3% | €63,600 |
| Partner | 60 | 25.0% | 75 | 22.7% | €0 |
| Outbound | 160 | 6.3% | 195 | 5.6% | €46,800 |

**Key observations:**

- **Paid search** dominates on volume (900 leads, +73% MoM) and attributed revenue (€238,800 in October). However, its lead-to-trial rate dropped from 8.5% to 6.4%, suggesting lead quality may be declining as spend scales. Three of its four October conversions are expansions, not new logos — expansions do not expand the customer base.
- **Partner** has the highest trial rate (23–25%) and delivered €166,800 in attributed revenue in September from just 60 leads. But it produced zero conversions in October. The channel is high-value but volatile and low-volume.
- **Content** has the most stable trial rate and produced two new SMB starters in October. Its attributed revenue per trial is the lowest, suggesting it skews toward smaller contracts.
- **Outbound** has the worst trial rate (5.6–6.3%) and lowest total attributed revenue across both months.

### 3.2 What cannot be concluded

**CAC payback by channel cannot be calculated.** The CFO's request requires channel spend data (ad budget, sales costs, tooling), which is not in this data pack. Without cost inputs, we can report attributed revenue and conversion volume, but not return on investment.

**Trial-to-paid conversion rate is not tracked.** The funnel only counts leads that reached a paid trial. The conversion from trial to paid customer runs on a longer cycle and is missing. We therefore cannot determine which channel is most efficient at producing paying customers per dollar spent.

**Attributed revenue ≠ recognised revenue.** Expansion bookings (Hooli in Sep, Stark/Umbrella/Globex/Massive in Oct) are attributed to the channel at conversion but take effect in the following billing cycle. This creates a timing mismatch between attribution and revenue recognition.

### 3.3 What to do

Before approving incremental spend, leadership should request:
1. Channel-level spend data (monthly budgets and actuals)
2. Full trial-to-paid conversion rates by channel
3. CAC by channel = (channel spend + allocated sales cost) ÷ new logos acquired
4. Payback period = CAC ÷ (monthly gross margin per new customer from that channel)

Without these, any spend increase is an uninformed bet.

### 4.3 Plan-by-plan assessment

**Enterprise — the revenue engine with eroding margins**
- Drives 87% of MRR but margin fell 2.7 points in one month.
- Infrastructure cost per customer rose 3% despite Acme's usage dropping 45% (batch migration). This means costs are scaling independently of usage — a structural efficiency problem.
- Support cost per customer rose 5.7% with no increase in customer count.
- Acme's migration proves usage can fall materially without revenue falling. The cost side has not followed.

**Pro — strongest economics, clearest growth signal**
- Best-in-class margin at 63%, roughly 2× Enterprise and 2× Starter.
- Highest plan utilisation (74%) — customers are approaching their included request limits. This creates a natural upgrade path to Enterprise.
- Per-customer GP fell 13%, largely because Nakatomi (€15K/mo) was replaced by Spacely (€3.2K/mo). This is a mix effect, not a structural decline.
- Two SMB pro customers (Wonka, Oscorp) generate high support ticket volume, which could be reduced with targeted product improvements.

**Starter — highest churn, lowest contribution**
- Gross profit per customer is just €792/month — support cost alone (€792) equals the per-customer gross profit.
- 4 of 5 October churners were starter customers. All had −35% to −37% usage declines in the prior month.
- Utilisation is only 42% — customers are barely using what they pay for, suggesting the plan may not be meeting their needs or they are not successfully adopting the product.
- Support cost per customer is disproportionately high relative to revenue: starter support cost per €1 of revenue is €0.43 vs. €0.25 for Enterprise and €0.13 for Pro.

### 4.4 Which plan deserves engineering focus

**Primary recommendation: Pro plan.**
- Highest margin (63%) means every euro saved or earned here has the most impact on contribution.
- 74% utilisation means product improvements that reduce per-request cost or increase capacity within existing infrastructure directly improve margins.
- Pro customers approaching limits are candidates for Enterprise upgrades, which would be the most valuable upsell motion (Enterprise GP per customer = €13,907 vs. Pro GP per customer = €4,611).

**Secondary priority: Enterprise cost optimisation.**
- Enterprise costs are rising while usage is flat. Engineering should investigate why infrastructure costs per customer increased 3% when aggregate Enterprise requests fell 1.4% (Acme's −45% partially offset by others' growth). This is likely a provisioning or autoscaling inefficiency.

**Starter plan should be deprioritised for product investment.** The plan's problems are more about customer success, onboarding, and fit than about product features. Consider automating support, tightening onboarding, or raising the plan price to improve unit economics.

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

### Decision 1: Reduce investment in SMB — **YES, proceed**

**Rationale:**
- SMB (14 active customers, all on Pro or Starter plans) generates €478,800 ARR = 5.3% of total active ARR.
- 4 of 5 October churners are SMB starters (€123,600 ARR lost).
- The churned customers showed −35% to −37% usage declines in September — warning signs that could have triggered intervention but apparently did not.
- Starter plan GP per customer (€792/mo) barely covers one support ticket (cost per ticket ≈ €2,526).
- New SMB starter logos (avg ARR ≈ €23,700) are not compensating for lost ones (avg ARR lost ≈ €30,900).

**Specific actions:**
1. Pause or reduce paid acquisition targeting SMB/starter segments.
2. Build automated usage-alert workflows for usage declines >20% — the churn pattern is predictable.
3. Evaluate whether the Starter plan should be sunset or restructured (higher minimum price, reduced support SLA, or self-serve only).
4. Reallocate freed resources to enterprise retention and pro-plan expansion motions.

### Decision 2: Increase acquisition channel spend — **NOT YET**

**Rationale:**
- We do not have channel spend data. Without cost, we cannot calculate ROI, CAC, or payback period.
- Paid search has the most volume and attributed revenue, but its lead-to-trial rate declined (8.5% → 6.4%), and 3 of 4 October conversions were expansions, not new logos.
- Partner channel has the best trial rate (25%) but zero October conversions — volatile and unscalable at current volume.
- The CFO specifically asked for CAC payback by channel, which is unanswerable with the current data.

**Immediate ask:** Request monthly channel spend data from marketing for September and October. Without this, any spend increase is an uninformed bet.

### Decision 3: Engineering focus — **Pro plan, with Enterprise cost workstream**

**Immediate priorities for next quarter:**
1. **Pro-plan capacity optimisation:** At 74% utilisation, small efficiency gains in request processing directly improve margins for the highest-margin plan. Build the upgrade path to Enterprise.
2. **Enterprise cost investigation:** Understand why infra costs rose 3% while aggregate usage was flat/falling. Fix provisioning or autoscaling.
3. **Starter onboarding automation:** Reduce support cost per starter customer through self-serve documentation, in-app guidance, and automated health alerts. This is a CS/automation problem, not a core product engineering problem.
