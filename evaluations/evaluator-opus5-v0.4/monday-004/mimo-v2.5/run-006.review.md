## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

### Decision 1: Should leadership reduce investment in a customer segment?

**Recommendation: Do not cut SMB investment broadly, but do reallocate within it. Do investigate Enterprise cost growth.**

- **SMB Starter:** The 44% churn rate is alarming, but net SMB MRR still grew (new logos outpaced lost ARR in October: +€133K new vs −€124K churned). The Starter plan is acquiring customers cheaply (low support/infra per customer) but retaining them poorly. The answer is not to cut SMB investment but to fix the onboarding and early-life experience. The fact that all four churned starters showed steep usage declines the month before churning suggests early-warning triggers exist and are not being acted on.
- **Enterprise:** The segment is not at risk of churn, but its cost structure is deteriorating. €17,300/month of margin erosion (€207,600 annualised) on the highest-revenue plan demands engineering investigation. This is not a "reduce investment" decision — it is an "invest in efficiency" decision.

### Decision 2: Should leadership increase spend on one acquisition channel?

**Recommendation: Increase partner investment cautiously. Do not increase paid search spend without addressing conversion-rate decline.**

- **Partner** is the most efficient new-logo channel (25% lead-to-trial, €83.4K average new-logo ARR per conversion). However, volume is low (75 leads/month). Determine whether partner volume can be scaled without diluting conversion quality.
- **Paid search** is scaling lead volume aggressively (+73%) but conversion efficiency is dropping (8.5% → 6.4%). Before increasing spend, investigate whether the additional leads are reaching the right audience and whether the trial experience is working for this cohort.
- **Content** is a solid middle ground (11% conversion, 3 new logos in 2 months, €30.4K average new-logo ARR per conversion).
- **Before any spend increase:** get the CAC payback data the CFO needs. This requires (a) channel spend data and (b) trial-to-paid conversion tracking. Without both, any spend decision is made in the dark.

### Decision 3: Which plan gets engineering focus?

**Recommendation: Enterprise plan — infrastructure efficiency.**

Rationale by scale of impact:
1. **Enterprise infrastructure efficiency:** If engineering can reduce Enterprise infra cost by 5% (€14,840/month, €178K/year), that is equivalent to eliminating the entire Starter plan's infrastructure cost twice over. Enterprise is 87% of revenue.
2. **Wonka/Pro rate-limit issues:** Wonka generated 8 support tickets in October, mostly rate_limits. They are likely hitting the 600K included-request ceiling. Engineering should investigate whether the Pro plan limits are well-calibrated. If Wonka is a typical Pro customer, rate-limit frustration could drive churn in the most profitable plan.
3. **Starter churn:** This is a product and customer-success problem more than an engineering problem. Improve trial-to-value experience and implement usage-based early-warning alerts (all four churned starters had >30% usage declines in the month before churn).

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

### Data Quality Problems

1. **Soylent duplicate invoice.** INV-2026-10-0209 appears twice — once from Stripe (2026-10-01, €19,500) and once from billing_sync (2026-10-02, €19,500). Same transaction ID, different sources. If both are counted, October revenue is inflated by €19,500. The two-source pattern suggests a replication/deduplication failure in the billing pipeline.

2. **Soylent September invoice discrepancy.** September invoice was €22,000, but current ARR (€234,000) implies €19,500/month. No overage should apply (980K requests vs 3M included). Possible explanations: recent contract renegotiation, prior-period adjustment, or billing error. Cannot determine cause from available data.

3. **Hooli September invoice discrepancy.** September invoice was €18,000, but current ARR (€252,000) implies €21,000/month. This is likely explained by the €36K expansion booking in September taking effect from the October billing cycle (consistent with stated policy), meaning September was invoiced at the pre-expansion rate (€252K − €36K = €216K → €18K/month). This appears correct.

4. **Gringotts annual invoice in October.** The €120,000 annual subscription (billing_period_months = 12) is legitimate but creates a one-time revenue recognition event. Monthly MRR impact is only €10,000. Financial reporting must defer €110,000 to future months.

5. **Plans.csv includes churned-during-month customers.** October's pro plan shows 9 customers despite Nakatomi churning on October 19. Mathematically, the count requires Nakatomi to be included (4 mid-market pro + 5 SMB pro = 9). November's plans.csv should exclude them. This is a consistent methodology (count all customers invoiced during the month) but could mislead readers expecting an end-of-month snapshot.

### Unsupported Causal Claims

6. **Cannot attribute SMB churn to any specific cause.** All four churned starters showed usage declines, but the data pack has no exit surveys, no renewal negotiation records, and no customer-health scores. The usage decline could be causal (customers reduced usage because they were leaving) or symptomatic (something else drove them away and usage dropped as a consequence). We cannot distinguish.

7. **Cannot attribute Enterprise cost growth to infrastructure over-provisioning.** The plans.csv attributes infrastructure cost "directly attributable to the plan," but we don't know the cost-allocation methodology. The per-request cost increase could reflect genuine over-provisioning, fixed-cost allocation across a slightly different customer mix, or data-centre pricing changes.

8. **Cannot determine whether paid-search expansion bookings are causally driven by paid-search campaigns.** Marketing attribution platforms commonly credit the last touch. These expansion bookings may have been driven by customer-success outreach or product improvements, with paid search merely being the last channel the buyer visited.

### Metrics That Cannot Be Calculated

9. **CAC payback by channel** — requires (a) channel-level spend data and (b) trial-to-paid conversion rates, neither of which is in this pack.

10. **Full-funnel conversion rates** — the funnel tracks lead → paid trial only. Trial → paid conversion "runs on a longer cycle and is not tracked." We cannot compute end-to-end customer acquisition efficiency.

11. **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)** — requires cohort-level tracking of expansion, contraction, and churn over trailing 12 months. We have only 2 months of data and no historical expansion/contraction records beyond current ARR.

12. **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)** — requires churn rates by cohort and segment, which cannot be reliably computed from a single month of churn data.

13. **Per-channel CAC** — as noted, no spend data exists. We can report attributed-revenue-per-lead as a rough efficiency proxy, but this is not CAC.

### Minor Observations

14. **Acme's usage decline is expected and should not alarm.** The 45% drop in interactive requests is consistent with the planned migration to batch processing (estimated 40–50% reduction). Token volume declined only 12% (4,620M → 4,066M), confirming the batch workload still processes substantial token volume through fewer API calls.

15. **Wonka Industries is hitting rate limits repeatedly** (5 rate_limits tickets across September and October). At 1,020K requests in October against a 600K included limit, they are 70% over their plan threshold. They should be engaged about upgrading to the Enterprise plan before this becomes a churn driver.
