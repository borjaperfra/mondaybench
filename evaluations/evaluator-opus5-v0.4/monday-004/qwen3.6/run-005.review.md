# Northstar Monday Business Review — Analysis & Recommendations

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
- **SMB/Starters are the weakest link:** The SMB segment (primarily the Starter plan) is experiencing a 44% monthly churn rate (4 of 9 starters churned in October), low usage relative to included limits, and support costs that are disproportionately high relative to ARR. **Recommendation: Reduce investment in this segment.**
- **Partner channel is the most efficient acquisition driver:** Despite lower volume, the Partner channel delivers a ~23% trial conversion rate (vs. 6–12% for other channels) and consistently brings in higher-ARR new logos. **Recommendation: Increase spend on the Partner channel.**
- **Starter plan requires immediate product focus:** Thin margins, high support load, and minimal overage revenue make the Starter plan the least sustainable. Engineering should prioritize retention, onboarding, and usage-growth features here.
- **Enterprise remains the profit engine but needs monitoring:** Revenue is stable, but support ticket volume is high and Acme’s usage dropped 45% (expected). Soylent’s unexplained 27% usage drop and critical tickets warrant immediate CS intervention.
- **CAC payback cannot be calculated:** Marketing spend data is absent from the pack. Finance must provide channel-level ad/ops budgets to compute CAC and payback periods.

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS
1. **Reduce investment in the SMB/Starters segment:** Reallocate CS and marketing resources away from cold outreach to Starter-tier prospects. Focus retention efforts on existing SMB accounts and consider raising the Starter price floor or bundling higher-value features to improve unit economics.
2. **Increase spend on the Partner channel:** It delivers the highest trial conversion rate (~23%) and consistently sources higher-ARR new logos. Scale partner incentives and co-marketing to offset low volume, while maintaining strict lead quality SLAs.
3. **Direct next quarter’s engineering focus to the Starter plan:** 
   - Build automated onboarding flows to reduce `how_to` and `integration` tickets.
   - Implement usage-growth triggers (e.g., smart rate-limit alerts, batch-job templates) to push usage toward included limits and create overage revenue.
   - Evaluate pricing/limit structure; consider introducing a usage-based top-up or migrating low-usage accounts to a lighter tier.
4. **Immediate CS intervention for Soylent:** 27% usage drop + 6 high/critical `api_errors` tickets in October signals technical distress or impending churn. Assign a dedicated CSM and engineering liaison this week.
5. **Finance/Marketing alignment:** Provide channel-level monthly spend to the Growth team so CAC and payback periods can be calculated. Until then, channel decisions should be guided by trial conversion efficiency and customer ARR quality.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

- **Funnel Metric Definition:** `funnel.csv` tracks leads reaching a *paid trial*, not closed deals. Trial-to-paid conversion is explicitly untracked, so true channel ROI is unknown.
- **Duplicate Invoice:** Soylent has two identical subscription invoices in October (`INV-2026-10-0209` from `stripe` and `billing_sync`). If not deduplicated, October revenue is overstated by €19,500.
- **Unexplained Usage Drops:** Soylent (-27%), Slate Rock (-34.7%), Mega Lo Mart (-37.1%), Dinoco (-35.5%), Buy N Large (-37.0%) all show sharp declines. The latter four are churned, but Soylent’s drop is unexplained and correlates with high-severity tickets.
- **Support Cost Allocation:** `support_cost_eur` is fully loaded but not broken down by ticket type or channel. It may not reflect marginal cost accurately, especially if CS headcount is fixed regardless of volume.
- **Acme’s Usage Shift:** The 45% drop is explained by batch migration, but the pack does not show whether batch requests are tracked in `usage.csv`. If batch traffic is excluded, actual platform utilization may be understated.
