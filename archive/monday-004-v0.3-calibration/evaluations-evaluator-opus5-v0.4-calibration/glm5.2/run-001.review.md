**Decision 2 — Acquisition channel to increase: content.**
- Most consistent new-logo output (3 logos across 2 months), stable ~10–12% trial conversion, and lead volume grew 27% without efficiency decay.
- Do **not** increase paid_search further this quarter — it already grew lead volume 73% with conversion down 1.9pp, and the Oct ACV headline is inflated by expansion attribution on existing enterprise accounts. Spend another dollar on paid_search only after the team produces a defensible spend ledger and confirms the expansion attribution is real.
- **Side-bet:** partner has the best economics per lead but only 75 leads/mo. If partner lead supply can be expanded (more referrals, more partner-sourced accounts), this is the highest-leverage small channel — pilot a partner-scale program in Q4.

**Decision 3 — Plan for engineering focus: Pro.**
- Rate-limits is a well-scoped product problem with measurable customer impact (10+ Oct tickets, 6 customers, recurring).
- The 600k included-requests ceiling is below actual usage for wonka (1.02M), aperture (705k), and vandelay (690k), all of whom should be on enterprise — the pro plan is currently blocking the upgrade path.
- Several rate-limited customers are *under* the limit, which means the rate-limit logic itself likely has a bug or a burst/concurrency dimension we don't understand. Engineering should diagnose before pricing changes.
- **Parallel SRE track (not product engineering):** enterprise reliability — 8 of 14 enterprise customers had high-severity api/performance tickets in Oct, support cost is rising faster than revenue, and soylent's critical ticket plus the downgrade is the leading indicator of a Mid-market bleed. 2. **CAC payback cannot be computed.** No marketing spend by channel is provided; the funnel stops at "paid trial" and trial-to-paid conversion is explicitly not in the pack. The CFO ask is unanswerable from this dataset.
3. **Expansion attribution is not trustworthy.** Three enterprise expansions in October (stark, umbrella, globex) are credited to paid_search campaigns that started 28 Sep 2026 — five days before conversion on accounts that have been customers since 2023–2024. Either the attribution model is retargeting-based or it is over-crediting; either way, do not budget against these as paid_search-driven outcomes.
4. **Soylent's downgrade is not in `acquisition.csv`.** Only hooli's September expansion is recorded. If downgrades are not being captured, the acquisition file overstates net new business and understates net revenue retention.
5. **Gringotts billing frequency change** (monthly → annual, €120k invoiced in Oct but only €10k counted in plan MRR) distorts cash-vs-MRR. Need a stated rule for how multi-month prepayments flow into MRR.
6. **Churned customers still paid full October invoices.** nakatomi (churned 19 Oct, €15k) and the 4 SMB churns all appear in October subscription revenue. Pro-rated refunds/credits, if any, are not visible. This means Oct MRR overstates November's run-rate by ~€25k.

8. **Acme overage revenue risk is not visible.** Acme was at 4.2M requests in Sept (over the 3M enterprise included allowance) and is now at 2.31M (under). If Acme was being billed overage at renewal, that revenue is now at risk; no overage line items are present in the revenue file.
9. **New Oct customers have no `usage_change_pct`** (spacely, cogswell, bishop, gekko, paper-street) — expected, but means MoM growth calculations are incomplete for the SMB cohort.
10. **`usage_account_id` ≠ `customer_id` for wayne (`wayne_ent`) and prestige (`prestige_ww`)** — joins must use the mapping field, not customer_id, or these customers' usage will silently drop out. Already handled here, but worth a pipeline check.
