# Northstar — Monday Business Review Analysis

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The October headline numbers are misleading. The main issue is not that the business suddenly grew; it is that revenue timing, a duplicate invoice, a professional-services decline, and churn are being mixed together.

**Most important findings:**

1. **October raw invoice revenue appears up 12.0%, but underlying revenue is weaker.**  
   - September raw invoiced revenue: **€797.1k**  
   - October raw invoiced revenue as loaded: **€892.9k**  
   - October raw revenue after removing the duplicate invoice: **€873.4k**  
   - October run-rate revenue, counting the annual invoice at monthly equivalent and excluding the duplicate: **€763.4k**  
   - So the apparent October increase is mostly an accounting/timing artifact, not underlying growth.

2. **Underlying subscription MRR grew only modestly, but current active MRR declined.**  
   - September subscription MRR: **€746.1k**  
   - October invoiced subscription MRR: **€757.7k**  
   - October current active MRR, excluding churned customers’ October invoices: **€732.4k**  
   - Current active MRR is therefore down **€13.7k** versus September, or **-1.8%**.

3. **Enterprise is stable and remains the core of the business.**  
   - Enterprise segment active ARR: **€6.78M**, about **77.1%** of active ARR.  
   - No Enterprise churn in October.  
   - Acme’s 45% request decline is explained by the batch migration note and is not evidence of product dissatisfaction or revenue risk.

4. **Mid-market had the largest single churn event.**  
   - Nakatomi churned on 2026-10-19 with ARR of **€180k**.  
   - Soylent is a red flag: usage down 27%, monthly revenue down €2.5k, and support volume spiked to 8 tickets including one critical ticket.

5. **SMB has high churn but also high new-logo replacement.**  
   - SMB churned ARR in October: **€123.6k** across four accounts.  
   - SMB new-logo ARR in October: **€133.2k** across five accounts.  
   - SMB active ARR still grew slightly, from **€469.2k** to **€478.8k**.  
   - However, SMB support intensity per revenue is much higher than Enterprise or Mid-market, and the Starter plan has weak unit economics.

6. **Acquisition channel conclusions are not supported.**  
   - Paid_search generated the most October attributed revenue, but **73% of its October attributed revenue was expansion bookings**, not new logos.  
   - There is no channel spend data, no trial-to-paid conversion tracking, and no cohort retention.  
   - Therefore, increasing spend on any channel is not supported by this pack.

7. **CAC payback by acquisition channel cannot be calculated reliably.**  
   - The CFO’s request cannot be answered from the supplied data. Missing inputs include channel cost, lead-to-paid conversion cohorts, actual realized revenue, retention, and gross margin by channel cohort.

8. **The plan with the strongest economics is Pro, and it deserves product engineering attention.**  
   - Pro has the highest contribution margin: **62.7%** in October.  
   - Pro usage grew **14.8%** in requests.  
   - Pro rate-limit support tickets increased from **5 in September to 13 in October**.  
   - Enterprise contributes the most absolute profit, but its margin declined and its issues look more commercial/account-management than product-engineering.  
   - Starter has low contribution per customer and low utilization; it is more a packaging/activation problem than a major engineering-focus problem.

### 3.3 CAC payback by acquisition channel

The CFO’s request cannot be answered from this pack.

CAC payback would require, at minimum:

```text
CAC payback months = CAC / monthly gross profit per customer
```

or a more complete cohort calculation using:

```text
CAC payback = cumulative gross profit required to recover CAC
```

Missing inputs:

1. **Channel spend**  
   No marketing cost, sales cost, or partner cost data is provided.

2. **Lead-to-trial-to-paid cohorts**  
   Funnel data tracks paid trials, not paid customers. Trial-to-paid conversion is explicitly not tracked.

3. **Actual realized revenue**  
   `attributed_revenue_eur` is annualized value at conversion, not invoiced or recognized revenue.

4. **Expansion timing**  
   Expansion bookings take effect from the following billing cycle. October expansions of €216.0k ARR equal €18.0k future MRR, but they are not in October subscription invoices.

5. **Retention and gross margin by channel cohort**  
   We have plan-level costs, but not channel-level customer cohorts or realized gross profit.

Therefore, any CAC payback number produced from this pack would be unsupported.

If both rows are loaded, October revenue is overstated by **€19.5k**.

- Deduplicate by transaction ID.
- Define which source is authoritative.
- Reconcile Stripe versus billing_sync.

### 6.2 Annual invoice timing

Gringotts invoice:

```text
INV-2026-10-0236, gringotts, 2026-10-01, annual_subscription, 12 months, €120,000
```

Revenue.csv records the full value in the month it is dated, but plans.csv counts annual contracts at monthly equivalent.

For run-rate analysis:

```text
€120.0k / 12 = €10.0k MRR
```

If leadership uses raw invoice revenue, October appears artificially strong by **€110k** versus monthly equivalent treatment.

### 6.3 Churned customers invoiced in October

Several customers churned during October but were invoiced on October 1:

### 6.4 Acquisition data is not ROI data

2. No CAC.

### 6.5 Segment profitability cannot be calculated exactly

- Segment-level support cost.
- Segment-level CS staffing cost.

So we can infer relative health, but we cannot calculate true segment profitability.

So overage potential is directional, not quantifiable.

`usage_account_id` is authoritative for linking billing to usage.

### 6.9 Limited historical data

The pack contains only September and October 2026.

Therefore:

- No August baseline.
- No long-term trend.
- No cohort retention beyond one month.
- No seasonality control.
- No multi-month channel conversion lag.

One month of churn, usage, or support movement is directional, not conclusive.

### 6.10 Support data lacks resolution quality metrics

We have:

- Ticket count.
- Severity.
- Category.
- Status.

We do not have:

- First response time.
- Resolution time.
- SLA breach.
- Customer satisfaction.
- Ticket reopen rate.
- Cost per ticket.

So support intensity can be measured, but support effectiveness cannot.
