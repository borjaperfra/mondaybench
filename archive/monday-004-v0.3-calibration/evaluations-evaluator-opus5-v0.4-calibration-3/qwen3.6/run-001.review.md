# Northstar Business Review: September–October 2026

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Northstar’s growth is being driven by the Enterprise segment, but profitability is eroding due to rising infrastructure costs and support overhead, particularly on the Enterprise plan. The SMB segment is showing signs of distress with high churn and low usage, while the Pro plan is the most efficient engine for net revenue contribution.**

Key findings:
*   **Revenue Stability:** Monthly subscription revenue remained flat between September (€646,100) and October (€657,700), despite significant churn in the SMB segment and new acquisitions. This stability is largely due to the Enterprise segment's dominance.
*   **Profitability Crisis:** The Enterprise plan’s gross margin dropped from 55.9% in September to 54.5% in October, driven by a disproportionate rise in infrastructure costs (likely linked to Acme Corp’s usage pattern change and overall volume growth). Support costs also rose sharply.
*   **SMB Distress:** The SMB segment lost 4 customers in October (churned) and gained 5 new ones, but the new customers have very low usage and low ARR. The "starter" plan is barely covering its support costs.
*   **Acquisition Inefficiency:** Paid Search is the highest volume channel but has the lowest conversion rate and lowest average deal size among new logos. Content marketing has the highest conversion rate but lower volume.
*   **Decision Support:**
    *   **Segment Investment:** Do **not** reduce investment in Enterprise yet, but do **not** invest further in SMB without a product-led growth fix. The focus should be on optimizing Enterprise margins.
    *   **Channel Spend:** Shift spend from Paid Search to Content Marketing for new logo acquisition. Paid Search is inefficient.
    *   **Product Focus:** The **Pro** plan deserves the most attention. It has the highest net margin, healthy usage growth, and is the bridge between SMB and Enterprise. The Enterprise plan needs cost optimization, not feature expansion.

The stability in total revenue masks significant churn and acquisition activity.

**Net Change Calculation:**
*   Start: €646,100
*   Less Churn: -€138,600
*   Plus New Logos: +€22,100
*   Plus Expansion: +€22,000
*   **Expected End:** €551,600
*   **Actual End:** €657,700

*Note: The discrepancy arises because the `revenue.csv` file includes invoices for the full month. The `customers.csv` shows current status. The "Expected End" calculation above assumes all churned customers stopped paying in October, which is correct for Nakatomi and the SMBs. However, the Enterprise segment's revenue is stable because the 8 Enterprise customers all renewed/continued. The "discrepancy" is actually due to the fact that the SMB churned customers' invoices were still issued in October before the churn date was recorded, or the churn date is the end of the billing period. Let's look closer.*

*   **SMB Churn Dates:** Slate Rock (Oct 8), Mega Lo Mart (Oct 15), Dinoco (Oct 22), Buy N Large (Oct 27). All invoices were issued on Oct 1. So they paid for October.
*   **Nakatomi Churn Date:** Oct 19. Invoice issued Oct 1. Paid for October.
*   **Therefore, the revenue in October includes payments from customers who churned during the month.**
*   **True Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) at end of October:**
    *   Enterprise: €659,500 (from `plans.csv`)
    *   Pro: €66,200 (from `plans.csv`)

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

1.  **CAC Payback Cannot Be Calculated:** The acquisition data does not include marketing spend. We can only calculate Revenue per Lead, not CAC or payback period.
2.  **Acme Corp’s Usage Drop:** The 45% drop in Acme’s usage is significant. The operational note says it was due to a move to batch runs. This is a one-time event, but it may signal a trend. If other Enterprise customers do the same, revenue may drop if not billed on usage.
3.  **Churn Dates vs. Invoice Dates:** The churn dates for SMB customers are in October, but invoices were issued on October 1. This means the revenue in October includes payments from churned customers. The true MRR at the end of October is lower than the invoice total.
4.  **Support Cost Attribution:** The support cost includes fully loaded cost of customer success staff. It is not clear if this cost is fixed or variable. If fixed, the margin calculation is accurate. If variable, the margin may be different.
5.  **Expansion Bookings:** The acquisition data shows expansion bookings, but the revenue data shows the actual invoiced amount. The expansion revenue in October (€22,000) is small compared to the total revenue. This suggests that expansion is not a major growth driver yet.
6.  **Data Quality:** The `usage.csv` file has missing `usage_change_pct` for some customers in October (e.g., Spacely Sprockets, Cogswell Cogs). This is expected for new customers, but it should be noted.
