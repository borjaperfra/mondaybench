# Northstar Business Review: September–October 2026

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Northstar is experiencing a structural shift in its Enterprise segment driven by a major customer (Acme Corp) optimizing usage, while SMB churn is accelerating. The "Enterprise" plan is currently the most profitable, but its growth is threatened by usage optimization. The "SMB/Starter" segment is bleeding customers with no visible acquisition pipeline to replace them. Growth is heavily reliant on "Paid Search," but the quality of these leads (SMB vs. Enterprise) is concerning.**

**Key Decisions Supported by Data:**
1.  **Do NOT reduce investment in the Enterprise segment yet.** While Acme's usage dropped, the segment remains the primary profit driver. However, support for Acme must be prioritized to prevent churn.
2.  **Do NOT increase spend on Paid Search immediately.** While it has the highest volume, the conversion value is low (SMB-heavy). The "Partner" channel has a higher conversion rate and brings in higher-value logos (Vandelay), but volume is low. A balanced approach is needed, but blind scaling of Paid Search is risky.
3.  **Product focus should be on the "Enterprise" plan.** Specifically, features that help customers manage and optimize usage (like Acme's batch processing) or prevent API errors (a major support driver) are critical. The "Starter" plan is losing money and customers; it needs a revamp or sunset strategy, not just "attention."

### 1. Customer Segment: Do NOT reduce investment in Enterprise.
*   **Reasoning:** Enterprise is 87% of revenue and 89% of gross profit. Reducing investment here would be catastrophic.
*   **Action:**
    *   **Acme Corp:** Assign a dedicated Customer Success Manager (CSM) to ensure the batch migration is successful and to explore upsell opportunities for their remaining usage. Their support tickets are high-severity; resolve them immediately.

    *   **Nakatomi:** Post-mortem the churn. High support ticket volume suggests product friction.

### 2. Acquisition Channel: Do NOT increase Paid Search spend yet.
*   **Reasoning:** Paid Search conversion rate is declining (8.5% -> 6.4%). It is driving SMBs who churn at 38%. We do not have CAC data to justify scaling.

    *   **Investigate Paid Search Quality:** Why is conversion dropping? Is the audience saturating?

    *   **Content:** Maintain spend. It has a stable 10%+ conversion rate.
    *   **CAC Payback:** Finance must calculate CAC by channel. If SMB CAC payback is >12 months due to churn, stop acquiring SMBs via Paid Search.

### 3. Product Plan: Focus on Enterprise and Pro, Revamp or Sunset Starter.
*   **Reasoning:** Starter has high support costs and high churn. Pro has the best margins. Enterprise is the revenue driver but needs support efficiency.
*   **Action:**
    *   **Enterprise:** Build features that help customers optimize usage (like Acme's batch processing) to reduce infra costs and increase satisfaction. Improve API error handling (major support driver).
    *   **Pro:** This is the growth engine. Invest in features that encourage expansion from Starter to Pro.
    *   **Starter:**
        *   **Option A:** Raise prices or reduce included requests to improve margins.
        *   **Option B:** Sunset the plan and force migration to Pro, which has better margins and lower support intensity per euro.
        *   **Option C:** If keeping, automate support (chatbots, docs) to reduce the €10.3k support cost.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

1.  **CAC Payback Cannot Be Calculated:** The `acquisition.csv` file provides `attributed_revenue_eur` but **no cost data** (lead cost, campaign spend). We cannot calculate CAC or payback period. This is a critical gap for the CFO's request.
2.  **Support Cost Attribution:** `support_cost_eur` in `plans.csv` is "directly attributable." However, support tickets in `support.csv` are not linked to plans. We are assuming the support costs in `plans.csv` are accurate. If support is shared, the profitability of Starter may be even worse.
3.  **Acme Corp's Future Revenue:** Acme's usage dropped 45%. If they are within their included quota, revenue is stable. If they are over quota, revenue may drop at renewal. We need to know their overage billing terms.
4.  **SMB Churn Cause:** We know SMBs are churning, but we don't know why. Is it price? Product? Support? The support data shows many "how_to" and "rate_limits" tickets for SMBs, suggesting product complexity or plan limits are a factor.
5.  **Expansion Revenue Attribution:** In `acquisition.csv`, expansion bookings are attributed to the channel. However, the `attributed_revenue_eur` for expansions (e.g., Stark €84k) is annualized. We need to know if this is a one-time expansion or recurring. The data says "Expansion bookings take effect from the following billing cycle," implying recurring. This is good, but we need to verify the actual revenue impact in `revenue.csv`. Stark's revenue in Oct was €95k (same as Sept), so the expansion may not have hit yet or was small.
