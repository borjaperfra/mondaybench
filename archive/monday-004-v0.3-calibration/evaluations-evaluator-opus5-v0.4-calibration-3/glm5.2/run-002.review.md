# Northstar — Monday Business Review

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The headline numbers look stable, but the underlying picture is deteriorating.**

- October MRR per the plans file was **€757,700** (€659.5k enterprise + €66.2k pro + €32.0k starter), up €11,600 vs. September. That headline is **flattered by invoicing timing**: October invoices were issued Oct 1–3, but **5 customers churned later in October** (after they'd already been invoiced for the month).
- **Gross churn in October: 5 customers, €303,600 ARR** — 1 mid-market pro customer (Nakatomi, €180k) + 4 SMB starter customers (€123.6k combined). That's **44% of September's starter customer base**.
- A further **€216,000 of expansion ARR was booked in October** (Stark, Umbrella, Globex, Massive, Cyberdyne) but **takes effect in November**, so it is not in October MRR.
- **Projected November MRR: ~€750,400**, i.e. a **net decline of ~€7,300 vs. October** even after the €18,000/mo of new expansion kicks in. Churn is currently outrunning expansion.
- Two enterprise customers are showing warning signs: **Soylent** (mid-market on the enterprise plan) downgraded by €30k ARR, dropped usage 27%, and opened 8 tickets in October (up from 3); **Acme** cut requests 45% via a planned batch-migration (in line with the 40–50% expectation, but a signal that enterprise customers can materially reduce API usage without reducing token volume).
- **Wayne Enterprises (€1.008M ARR) renews Nov 30** — the third-largest account and a critical renewal this month.

**Decisions supported by the data:**
1. **Reduce investment in SMB, specifically the SMB/starter sub-segment** — high churn, low contribution per customer, support cost disproportionate to revenue.
2. **Increase spend on the Partner acquisition channel** — best economics on every measure for new logos, but with a caveat about scalability (see §3).
3. **Focus engineering on the Enterprise plan** — largest revenue and contribution at risk, declining margins, widespread api_errors/performance issues, and a warning sign from Acme that enterprise customers can optimize usage downward.

**CFO ask — CAC payback by channel cannot be calculated from this pack** (no marketing spend data). See §6.

### Expansion attribution (separate concern)

Paid search is also credited with €210,000 of expansion ARR (Hooli €36k, Stark €84k, Umbrella €48k, Globex €42k). But Stark, Umbrella, and Globex have been Northstar customers since 2023–2024 — the causal claim that paid search "acquired" their expansion is hard to defend and likely reflects an over-generous last-touch attribution model. Treating that expansion as paid-search acquisition performance would be misleading.

### What can and cannot be concluded

**Can conclude:**
- Partner has the strongest observed economics for **new logo** acquisition.
- Paid search has the highest volume but the worst efficiency (lowest trial rate, lowest ACV per lead).
- The funnel "conversions" are to paid trial, not to closed-won — the relationship between trials and the logos in `acquisition.csv` involves an untracked lag.

**Cannot conclude:**
- Whether Partner can scale beyond ~70 leads/month — we don't know if partner inventory is spend-constrained or partner-capacity-constrained.
- Whether paid search "drove" the enterprise expansions or merely touched them.
- True CAC or payback period (no spend data).

### Recommendation on the channel decision
Increase spend on **Partner**, but **tiered**: start with a 25–50% budget test to validate that lead volume scales with spend before committing to a large increase. Partner's economics are clearly best, but its volume ceiling is unknown.

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

### Decision 1 — Finance: reduce investment in SMB, specifically SMB/starter
**Supported.** The SMB segment overall isn't uniformly broken (SMB pro: 5 stable customers, €218.4k ARR), but the **starter sub-segment** is in a churn spiral:
- 44% monthly churn from the September base
- €792/mo contribution per customer
- Support cost = 32% of revenue
- Net MRR contribution **negative** after churn
- October's new starter ACV (€94.8k) replaced churned ACV (€123.6k) at lower quality

**Action:** Reduce paid acquisition targeting SMB/starter. Tighten qualification — the 4 churned customers all showed declining usage for the full month before churn, which is a leading indicator we can screen for. Continue SMB/pro acquisition (Spacely, Planet Express, Wonka are stable and growing).

### Decision 2 — Growth: increase spend on Partner (with a scalability test)
**Supported, conditionally.** Partner has the best economics on every new-logo measure (€83.4k ACV/logo, 23.7% trial rate, €1,236 ACV/lead). However, partner lead volume is small (135 leads over 2 months) and we don't know whether the ceiling is spend or partner capacity.

**Action:** Increase partner spend by 25–50% as a controlled test. Measure whether lead volume scales linearly. Do **not** reallocate from content (decent economics) to do this — the natural source is paid search, which has the worst efficiency.

**Do not** treat paid-search's expansion attribution (€210k) as a reason to invest more in paid search — those expansions are on customers acquired years ago and the attribution is questionable.

### Decision 3 — Product: focus engineering on the Enterprise plan
**Supported.** Enterprise is:
- The largest revenue (€659.5k MRR) and contribution (€194.7k) base — the most absolute value at risk
- Showing **declining margins** (32.2% → 29.5%) — cost growth outrunning revenue
- Carrying **disproportionate support cost** (€12,000/customer vs. €955 for pro) — partly driven by recurring api_errors and performance tickets across multiple enterprise accounts
- Displaying **value-perception warning signs** (Acme's batch migration; customers using only 63% of included requests)
- About to lose visibility on Wayne Enterprises (€1.008M ARR) if the Nov 30 renewal isn't actively protected

**Action:** Engineering focus on (a) reducing api_errors/performance ticket load on enterprise accounts, (b) understanding why enterprise utilization is at 63% and whether the included-request ceiling / overage pricing is misaligned, (c) building renewal-protection features ahead of Wayne's Nov 30 renewal. Secondary focus: Pro retention (post-Nakatomi).

### CFO ask — CAC payback by acquisition channel
**Cannot be delivered from this pack.** No marketing spend data is included — `acquisition.csv` carries attributed revenue but not cost. We can produce:
- ACV per channel (above)
- Annualized contribution per new logo by channel (using plan margins and the new logo's plan)
- A plan-level gross margin per plan

But not a payback period. **Required to compute it:** monthly marketing spend per channel. Without that, any "payback" number would be invented. Recommend the Growth team supplement this pack with spend data so we can compute CAC, CAC payback, and ROI by channel next week.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

1. **Duplicate Soylent invoice in October.** `INV-2026-10-0209` appears twice — once from `stripe` dated 2026-10-01 and once from `billing_sync` dated 2026-10-02 — same amount (€19,500), same invoice ID. The plans file counts it once (correct), but summing `revenue.csv` naively would double-count. Needs de-duplication logic on invoice_id.

2. **CAC payback cannot be calculated.** No marketing spend per channel in the pack. CFO ask is unanswerable as currently scoped.

3. **Expansion attribution to paid search is unreliable.** Paid search is credited with €210k of expansion from customers (Stark, Umbrella, Globex, Hooli) acquired in 2023–2024. Last-touch attribution is almost certainly over-crediting paid search for expansions it didn't meaningfully drive. Any decision to increase paid search spend on the basis of "expansion performance" would be unsupported.

4. **Gringotts moved to annual billing in October** (€120,000 invoice for 12 months). This is a one-time €120k cash inflow in October but does not change MRR (still €10k/month). Cash and MRR pictures diverge for this customer.

5. **`plans.csv` October starter "customers" = 13** includes the 4 that churned later in the month (they were invoiced Oct 1 then churned Oct 8–27). True end-of-month active starter count is **9**, and active MRR is **€21,700**, not €32,000. Headline plan growth overstates starter health.

6. **Funnel "conversions" are to paid trial, not to closed-won.** Trial-to-paid conversion "is not tracked in this pack." So we cannot directly link funnel trial counts to `acquisition.csv` logo counts — there's an unmeasured lag and an unmeasured conversion step in between.

7. **`support_cost_eur` is described as fully-loaded CS staff cost, not ticket-driven.** Enterprise support cost rose €9,000 month-over-month on only +2 tickets — consistent with a headcount add, not ticket volume. Don't interpret support cost changes as direct ticket-cost changes.

8. **Churned customers retain their pre-churn `arr_eur` in `customers.csv`.** Nakatomi (churned) still shows €180,000 in the file. Any ARR roll-up must exclude `status = churned` or it will overstate the book by €303,600.

9. **No causal claim can be made** about why the 4 SMB starter customers churned (no exit surveys). The usage decline is *correlated* with churn, not necessarily *causal* — but it's the best leading indicator we have.

10. **Plan-level contribution margins are gross of any S&M / R&D / G&A not in `infra_cost` or `support_cost`.** True unit economics are weaker than the contribution figures shown — these are plan-attributable costs only.
