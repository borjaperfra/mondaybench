## CEO BRIEFING

Dana — here's the state of play ahead of your 15:00 board call.

**What happened.** At 11:42 UTC today, the Batch API — one of our four independent API surfaces — began returning 502 errors at elevated rates. The other three surfaces (Inference, Embeddings, Files) were unaffected throughout. The errors were confined to one of the Batch API's two worker fleets.

**Who is hit.** Of 36 customer accounts, 9 use the Batch API. Of those, 7 experienced failures. About 17,600 Batch API requests failed during the incident window (11:42–12:58 UTC) out of roughly 58,900 Batch API requests in that window — approximately 30% of Batch API traffic. No other API surface was affected.

**What we've done.** At 12:58 UTC we shifted all Batch API traffic to the unaffected fleet. Error rates dropped immediately to near-baseline levels. We are now in a 90-minute verification window that closes at 14:30. As of 14:20 the error rate is holding steady at 0.3–0.4% against a baseline of 0.2%.

**Current state.** Mitigated but not resolved. We cannot declare resolution until the verification window closes clean at 14:30. We are also re-driving the backlog of failed batch jobs for affected accounts — about 60% complete, no failures on re-drive so far.

**Root cause.** Not yet established. We have two live hypotheses and have deliberately held off testing them to preserve evidence while we stabilised. Root-cause investigation will begin after the window closes.

**What to say if it comes up.** "An incident on our Batch API began at 11:42 UTC. We mitigated it at 12:58 by shifting traffic to an unaffected fleet. We are monitoring now and expect to confirm resolution shortly. Seven customer accounts saw failed requests; no other Northstar API was impacted."

**Relationship risk.** Contoso, an enterprise customer on an SLA contract, has written to support twice asking about service credits. Under their contract, credits are determined at month end from our published availability figure — we don't commit to credits mid-month — but we should prioritise a prompt, transparent follow-up with them.

We deliberately did not roll back `r3` (decision at 12:31). Rolling it back would remove fleet-a state needed to test the hypotheses. Traffic was cut to fleet-b instead.
