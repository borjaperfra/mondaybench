## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Headline metrics for October 2026 appear reassuring on the surface—recognized plan MRR increased from **€746.1k** to **€757.7k**, and invoiced cash jumped due to an upfront annual payment. However, a deeper look reveals critical underlying risks, misleading attribution, and immediate operational priorities:

1. **The November Revenue Cliff:** October was hit by **5 customer churns totaling €303,600 in ARR (€25,300 MRR)**. Because subscriptions were invoiced on October 1st before customers churned mid-month, **zero** of this revenue loss appears in October’s billed numbers. It will hit financials in November.
2. **Decision 1 — Customer Segment Investment:** **Do not execute an indiscriminate cut to the SMB segment.** While the low-tier **Starter plan** (all SMB) is failing (31% logo churn in October, forward gross margin near 0%), SMB accounts on the **Pro plan** (e.g., Wonka Industries) are expanding usage rapidly and generate high margins. Investment should be removed specifically from the self-serve **Starter plan**, while actively defending **Mid-Market** accounts, where a single churn (Nakatomi, €180k ARR) erased more revenue than all SMB churns combined.
3. **Decision 2 — Acquisition Channel Spend:** **Do not increase spend on Paid Search.** The perceived surge in Paid Search performance (+€238.8k in attributed bookings) is an attribution artifact: **€174k (73%)** of that total came from contract expansions on existing multi-year Enterprise accounts (Stark Industries, Umbrella Health, Globex). Furthermore, lead-to-trial conversion on Paid Search deteriorated from **8.46% to 6.44%**. Crucially, **CAC payback cannot be calculated** because marketing spend is absent from company reporting.
4. **Decision 3 — Product Plan Engineering Focus:** Engineering focus next quarter must be allocated to the **Pro Plan**. Pro is Northstar’s most profitable tier (**62.7% gross margin**, compared to 29.5% for Enterprise and 32.2% for Starter), but customers are hitting a hard ceiling. Pro rate-limit support tickets surged from 5 in September to 13 in October as top accounts exceeded the 600k request cap. Engineering must deliver automated burst capacity, dynamic overage billing, and bridging tiers to prevent churn and unlock expansion.

### Key Economic Insights

1. **The Pro Plan is Northstar’s Economic Engine:** Generating **~63% gross margin**, Pro is twice as profitable as Enterprise. However, Pro customers are running into rigid capacity constraints. The plan includes **600,000 requests/month**. In October:
   - **Wonka Industries** hit **1,020,000 requests** (exceeded limit by 70%; raised 4 rate-limit tickets).
   - **Aperture Labs** hit **705,000 requests** (raised 2 rate-limit tickets).
   - **Vandelay** hit **690,000 requests** (raised 1 rate-limit ticket).
   - **Support bottleneck:** Rate-limit support tickets across Pro jumped from 5 in September to **13 in October**. Because usage beyond the plan is billed only at renewal, Northstar absorbs the immediate compute expense without immediate revenue capture, while frustrating expanding clients.
2. **Enterprise Margins are Unhealthy:** Despite contributing 87% of monthly revenue, Enterprise delivers a sub-30% gross margin due to heavy direct compute costs (€296.8k) and dedicated Customer Success staff (€168k, averaging €12k per customer/month).
3. **The Starter Plan Collapse:** Starter shows an apparent 32.2% gross margin in October, but this plan carries severe churn. In November, MRR will fall by €10,300 to **€21,700**. If infrastructure and support costs do not instantly scale down by 45%, **Starter will operate at near 0% gross margin or a net loss**.

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

### Decision 1: Customer Segment Strategy (Finance Inquiry)
* **Do NOT disinvest from SMB as an entire category.** High-growth SMB accounts on Pro (Wonka, Oscorp, Pied Piper) are healthy, high-margin, and expanding.
* **Deprecate or Automate the Starter Plan:** Discontinue dedicated CS allocation for the Starter plan immediately. Transition Starter to a 100% self-serve, unassisted tier with strict automated guardrails. If Starter cannot operate profitably on automated infrastructure, sunset the plan entirely.
* **Form an Urgent Mid-Market SWAT Team:** Mid-Market represents €1.53M in active ARR but is our greatest immediate risk. Deploy dedicated engineering support to Soylent to resolve API/performance failures before it churns, and conduct executive reviews across the remaining 9 Mid-Market accounts.

### Decision 2: Acquisition Channel Spend (Growth Inquiry)
* **Reject the Proposed Spend Increase on Paid Search:** Paid Search is experiencing diminishing lead-to-trial conversion, and its apparent ARR generation is an artifact of credited enterprise expansions.
* **Audit Marketing Attribution Logic:** Reconfigure the attribution model to differentiate between new logo acquisition and account expansion. Existing accounts clicking search ads must be routed to account management and excluded from channel acquisition ROI.
* **Invest in the Partner Channel:** Partner produces the highest trial conversion rates (>22%) and generated Northstar’s largest new logo deal in September (Vandelay, €132k ARR). Channel investments should prioritize partner co-marketing and integration ecosystems.

### Decision 3: Engineering Focus (Product Inquiry)
* **Dedicate Next Quarter’s Engineering to the Pro Plan:**
  1. **Automated Usage & Overages:** Replace static rate limits with soft caps, automatic tiered throttling notifications, and automated in-month overage billing to replace manual renewal reconciliation.
  2. **Introduce an "Enterprise-Lite" / Pro-Scale Tier:** Wonka (1.02M requests) and Aperture (705k requests) have outgrown the 600k Pro limit but are not yet ready for a €50k+/month Enterprise commitment. Create a bridge tier (e.g., 1.5M requests at €8k–€10k/month) to capture expansion revenue naturally.
* **Secondary Focus — Platform Batching:** Productize the batch processing pattern proven by Acme Corp across other Enterprise workloads to lower compute footprints and improve Enterprise plan gross margins.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

During this analysis, six major data discrepancies and structural reporting gaps were identified:

1. **Missing Spend Data (CFO Request Unexecutable):** There is no cost, marketing expense, or sales headcount data in `funnel.csv`, `acquisition.csv`, or any other table. CAC and CAC payback cannot be calculated.
2. **Double Invoicing Glitch (`revenue.csv`):** Transaction ID `INV-2026-10-0209` for Soylent appears twice:
   - `2026-10-01, subscription, 1 month, €19,500, source: stripe`
   - `2026-10-02, subscription, 1 month, €19,500, source: billing_sync`  
   If both records are ingested into accounting, October cash revenue is overstated by **€19,500**.
3. **Flawed Expansion Attribution:** Campaigns launched on 2026-09-28 are claiming credit for contract expansions on customers that joined in 2023 and 2024 (Stark Industries, Umbrella Health, Globex, Cyberdyne). This systematically skews channel ROI toward whichever channel existing users happen to click when accessing the service.
4. **Uncaptured Accrued Revenue:** `plans.csv` states that request volume exceeding plan limits is billed separately at renewal. Accounts such as Wonka, Aperture, and Vandelay ran significant overages in October, but no accrued unbilled revenue is tracked in the monthly reporting pack.
5. **Database Identifier Inconsistencies:** In `customers.csv`, Wayne Enterprises uses `usage_account_id` = `wayne_ent` and Prestige Worldwide uses `prestige_ww`. While mapped in this review, non-standard naming conventions between billing and telemetry pipelines pose an ongoing risk of pipeline join failures.
6. **Funnel Cohort Lag:** Funnel conversions represent leads converting to paid trials within the same calendar month, but trial-to-paid subscription cycles are tracked on a separate, unprovided timeline. Funnel conversions cannot be directly correlated to new logo subscription revenue in the same monthly period.
