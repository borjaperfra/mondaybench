## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Northstar’s billing headline overstates growth, while advance invoicing masks customer losses.**

- Raw invoice value rose **12.0%**, but after removing a duplicate, annual-billing timing and professional services, monthly-equivalent subscription revenue grew only **1.6%**.
- October invoices still include five customers who subsequently churned. Excluding their contracts, the active-customer recurring run rate is **€732,400/month**, **1.8% below September**.
- **A blanket segment investment cut is not supported.** SMB has serious churn, but retained SMB customers are growing usage. Enterprise’s request decline is largely explained by Acme’s planned migration to batching—not broad disengagement.
- **No acquisition-channel spending increase is justified yet.** Paid search’s attributed revenue is predominantly expansion at existing customers, and the pack contains no acquisition spending.
- **Enterprise-plan cost efficiency is the largest immediate economic priority.** Pro has the strongest direct contribution margin, but rising costs and account-level usage limits warrant targeted attention.

**Enterprise is not broadly disengaging.**

- Enterprise-segment requests fell **1.9%**, from 22.0m to 21.591m.

October’s three tickets were all resolved, with no high-severity tickets. **SMB needs retention diagnosis, not indiscriminate withdrawal.** Among September SMB customers still active at October-end, requests grew from **1.845m to 2.129m: +15.4%**, excluding churned customers and October additions.

Wayne also deserves renewal attention: its **€1.008m ARR** renews November 30, and October includes pending high-severity performance and open integration tickets.

## 3. ACQUISITION

### Separate new-customer value from expansion attribution

Across both months:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Paid search | 2 | €64,800 | €210,000 | €274,800 |
| Partner | 2 | €166,800 | €0 | €166,800 |
| Content | 3 | €91,200 | €18,000 | €109,200 |
| Outbound | 2 | €56,400 | €24,000 | €80,400 |

**76.4% of paid search’s credited value is expansion**, not new-logo acquisition. Those expansion customers predate the campaigns, so attribution does not demonstrate that advertising caused the bookings.

Partner has the largest observed new-logo contract value, but only two wins, both in September. That is insufficient evidence of scalable superiority.

Expansion timing also matters:

- September’s €36,000 Hooli expansion explains October’s €3,000 monthly increase.
- October expansion bookings total **€216,000 annualized**, equivalent to **€18,000/month**, effective from the following billing cycle. They are not October recurring revenue and should not be silently added to the current-contract snapshot.

### Funnel “conversions” are paid trials—not contracted customers

| Channel | September lead → paid-trial rate | October rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Paid search | 8.46% | 6.44% |
| Content | 11.92% | 10.30% |
| Partner | 25.00% | 22.67% |
| Outbound | 6.25% | 5.64% |
| **Overall** | **10.00%** | **8.00%** |

Paid-search leads increased **73.1%**, but paid trials increased only **31.8%**. All channels’ same-month trial rates declined.

This shows weaker same-month lead-to-trial performance—not necessarily worse eventual customer acquisition. Trial-to-customer conversion and conversion timing are missing. Dividing acquisition wins by the same month’s funnel leads would mix unmatched cohorts.

**CAC payback cannot be calculated.** There is no channel spending, allocated acquisition labor cost or acquired-cohort contribution history.

A suitable framework is:

> **CAC payback months = acquisition cost per new customer ÷ monthly contribution per acquired customer.**

Annualized attributed revenue is not monthly contribution, and expansion bookings must be separated from new-logo acquisition.

## 4. PLAN ECONOMICS

Define direct contribution as:

> **Plan recurring revenue − infrastructure cost − support cost**

Support already includes assigned customer-success staff; do not subtract that cost again.

| Plan | September contribution | September margin | October contribution | October margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Enterprise | €212,000 | 32.2% | €194,700 | 29.5% |
| Pro | €42,500 | 67.5% | €41,500 | 62.7% |
| Starter | €8,400 | 34.9% | €10,300 | 32.2% |
| **Total** | **€262,900** | **35.2%** | **€246,500** | **32.5%** |

Despite recurring revenue growth, contribution fell **€16,400, or 6.2%**.

### Enterprise: largest economic exposure

Enterprise’s direct costs rose **€17,800**, while revenue rose just €500. Its **€17,300 contribution decline** exceeds the company’s net decline because Starter partly offsets it.

It has the lowest October percentage margin, but still delivers approximately **79% of total direct contribution**. Cutting it simply because its margin percentage is lowest would ignore its absolute value.

### Pro: strongest margin, with growing usage and packaging pressure

Pro remains strongest on percentage margin. However:

- Revenue increased €3,200, while direct costs increased €4,200.
- Tickets increased **19 → 28**.
- Rate-limit tickets increased **5 → 13**.

The average October usage—441,889 requests—is below the 600,000 allowance, but conceals three accounts above it:

- Aperture: **705,000**, 17.5% above allowance
- Vandelay: **690,000**, 15.0% above
- Wonka: **1,020,000**, 70.0% above

These are candidates for capacity, packaging and renewal-overage review. However, rate-limit tickets also occur below the monthly allowance, so monthly volume alone cannot explain them. Overage rates and renewal collections are unavailable.

### Starter: profitable on supplied direct costs, but retention is weak

Starter contribution increased despite margin compression. Its average requests fell **26.3%**, but that average mixes four low-volume new customers with customers who churned during October.

The five September Starter customers who remained active grew requests from **520,000 to 613,000: +17.9%**.

Thus, the falling average does not establish falling engagement among retained customers. Nevertheless, four of nine opening Starter customers churned—**44.4%**—which requires urgent investigation.

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Do not approve a blanket segment cut.** Investigate SMB/Starter churn and Mid-market revenue loss separately. Plan-level costs cannot establish segment profitability because Enterprise and Pro span multiple segments.
2. **Do not broadly increase channel spending yet.** Obtain channel costs, separate expansion from acquisition, and track matched cohorts through contracted-customer conversion and retention. Test incremental channel impact before scaling.
3. **Make Enterprise cost efficiency and reliability the primary engineering priority on current economic evidence.** It offers the largest identified contribution problem. Establish cost drivers before assuming savings are achievable.
4. **Give Pro a targeted capacity-and-packaging workstream.** Protect its strong margins, investigate rate-limit friction and review high-volume accounts before renewal.
5. **Act immediately on Soylent and upcoming renewals; investigate churn reasons.** Monitor Acme using tokens, users and workload outcomes, not request count alone.
6. **Replace the headline dashboard** with deduplicated billings, normalized recurring revenue, active exit run rate, recurring-revenue movements and direct contribution.

The data identifies priorities, but does not establish which engineering project has the highest return without effort estimates and expected impact.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

- **Duplicate invoice:** Soylent’s transaction appears in two source systems with different dates. Deduplicate by transaction ID and resolve source precedence.
- **Identity joins:** Use `customers.usage_account_id`, including `wayne_ent` and `prestige_ww`; joining usage directly to customer IDs would omit them.
- **Population mismatch:** October plan counts include customers billed before churn. They are not month-end active-customer counts.
- **Current versus historical ARR:** Churned rows retain positive ARR. Summing all customer ARR overstates the active base; the current snapshot also cannot substitute for historical contracts.
- **Billing is not cash collection or accounting recognition:** Payment status, refunds, credits and recognition schedules are absent.
- **Churn-month usage is not exposure-adjusted:** Lower October usage for churned customers may partly reflect shorter service periods, not advance warning.
- **Costs are incomplete for company profitability:** Direct contribution excludes unspecified shared overhead and acquisition costs. Customer- and segment-level cost allocations are unavailable.
- **Support counts lack effort and timing:** Ticket severity, status and volume do not reveal resolution hours or prove causes of churn, contraction or infrastructure costs.
- **Two months and nine new logos are a small sample.** Sustainable channel performance, lifetime value, long-term retention and causal effects cannot be established.
