### Funnel “conversions” are paid trials, not acquired customers

| Channel | September lead → paid trial | October lead → paid trial |
|---|---:|---:|
| Paid search | 44 / 520 = **8.46%** | 58 / 900 = **6.44%** |

Every channel’s same-month trial rate declines. Paid search’s leads rise **73.1%**, but trials rise only **31.8%**.

These are descriptive early-funnel results—not mature lead-to-customer conversion rates. Acquisition records cannot simply be divided by the same month’s funnel leads because conversion cycles span months.

### CAC payback cannot be calculated

Missing inputs include channel spend, fully loaded acquisition costs, matched customer cohorts and their realised recurring contribution.

A defensible calculation would be:

> **CAC payback months = acquisition cost per new customer ÷ monthly contribution per acquired customer**

Annualised attributed bookings are not monthly contribution, and paid trials are not the required new-customer denominator.

### Enterprise: greatest absolute value and biggest deterioration

Enterprise generates **79.0% of October contribution**, but its direct costs rise **€17,800** against only **€500** of revenue growth.

It has the lowest October percentage margin, but cutting it indiscriminately would threaten the largest contribution pool. Investigate infrastructure efficiency, reliability and support demand.

Importantly, **Enterprise segment and enterprise plan are not interchangeable**: six Mid-market customers use the enterprise plan.

### Pro: strongest percentage economics, emerging capacity pressure

Pro retains the highest margin, **62.7%**, but costs grow faster than revenue.

The average of **441,889 requests per customer** is below the 600,000 allowance, yet masks three customers above it:

- Wonka: **1,020,000**, or **170%** of allowance
- Aperture: **705,000**, or **117.5%**
- Vandelay: **690,000**, or **115%**

Together they exceed their individual allowances by **615,000 requests**. Pro rate-limit tickets increase **5 → 13**.

Prioritise investigation of rate limits, workload visibility and upgrade/overage handling. Do not assume rate-limit tickets necessarily arise from monthly allowance exhaustion: customers below their allowance also report them.

### Starter: retention and service economics need repair

Starter’s October contribution is positive, but support absorbs **32.2% of revenue** and its contribution margin trails Pro substantially.

Average usage falls **26.3%**, but the denominator includes four low-usage new customers and four customers who churn during October. The five surviving September Starter customers grow combined requests from **520,000 to 613,000, +17.9%**.

Thus, falling average usage is not evidence that all Starter customers are disengaging.

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Reject a broad segment-investment cut this week.** Investigate Starter retention and onboarding rather than treating all SMB customers alike. Segment-level profitability is unavailable.
2. **Intervene with Soylent immediately.** Combine technical remediation with a commercial review. Prepare Wayne’s November 30 renewal, given its €1.008m ARR and unresolved October integration/performance tickets.
3. **Make Enterprise cost-to-serve and reliability the primary engineering investigation.** It is the largest contribution pool and the source of the overall contribution decline. Validate specific savings opportunities before committing the entire quarter.
4. **Give Pro a targeted capacity and monetisation workstream.** Protect its strong margins and growing accounts; measure whether changes reduce support costs and improve retention or upgrades.
5. **Do not scale paid search from attributed revenue alone.** Separate expansion from acquisition and instrument spend, mature cohorts and incremental lift. Partner merits further testing, not an assumption of superior ROI.
6. **Review exit MRR, churn and contribution alongside invoice totals.** Those measures reveal deterioration that headline billings conceal.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

- **Duplicate invoice:** Soylent’s repeated transaction ID inflates October by €19,500.
- **Billing timing:** annual prepayment and non-recurring services distort monthly comparisons.
- **Customer snapshots:** churned customers retain nonzero ARR. Summing every customer’s ARR overstates active contracted value.
- **Plan counts are not month-end active counts:** October lists 36 customers, including five who churned. Exit counts are **14 enterprise, 8 Pro and 9 Starter**.
- **Forward bookings:** October expansion bookings total **€216,000 annualised**, equivalent to €18,000 monthly if effective as stated. They belong to the following billing cycle, not October revenue. Their absence from the “current” customer ARR snapshot needs reconciliation.
- **Usage joins:** using customer IDs directly would omit Wayne and Prestige.
- **Causality is unproven:** support tickets do not establish churn causes; marketing attribution does not establish incrementality.
- **Economics are partial:** plan contribution excludes unlisted company costs. Account-level cost, overage prices and collection outcomes are missing, so customer profitability and future overage contribution cannot be established.
- **Only two months are supplied:** trends, seasonality, durable retention, channel scalability and reliable CAC payback remain unproven.
