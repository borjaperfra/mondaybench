## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

- **The apparent revenue growth is mostly a billing artefact.** Raw invoice totals rise **12.0%**, but include a duplicate and an annual prepayment. After deduplication and monthly normalisation, recurring revenue rises only **1.6%**.
- **October billing conceals churn later in the month.** Five customers leave, removing **€25,300 monthly recurring revenue**. Based on active contracts, the exit run rate is **€732,400/month**, down **1.8%** from September.
- **A broad segment cut is not supported.** Enterprise demand is healthier than request counts suggest. SMB has serious Starter churn, but new customers offset its lost recurring revenue. Mid-market has the clearest revenue deterioration.
- **Paid search has not demonstrated superior acquisition economics.** Most of its October attributed value comes from existing-customer expansion. No channel spend or matched customer cohorts are supplied, so CAC and CAC payback cannot be calculated.
- **Enterprise deserves the largest cost-efficiency focus; Pro deserves targeted product work.** Enterprise generates the most contribution but drives its decline. Pro has the highest contribution margin, alongside growing rate-limit friction.

### CFO request: CAC payback is not calculable

Required inputs are missing:

- Channel spend and attributable acquisition costs
- Cohort-matched acquired customers
- Monthly contribution from those acquired customers over time

The relevant calculation is:

> **CAC payback months = acquisition cost ÷ monthly contribution from the acquired cohort**

Annualised attributed revenue is neither acquisition cost nor monthly contribution. Expansion bookings must also be separated from new-logo acquisition.

## 4. PLAN ECONOMICS

Define **direct contribution = recurring revenue − infrastructure cost − support cost**. Support already includes assigned customer-success staff.

| Plan | September contribution | September margin | October contribution | October margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Enterprise | €212,000 | 32.2% | €194,700 | 29.5% |
| Pro | €42,500 | 67.5% | €41,500 | 62.7% |
| Starter | €8,400 | 34.9% | €10,300 | 32.2% |
| **Total** | **€262,900** | **35.2%** | **€246,500** | **32.5%** |

Despite recurring revenue increasing **€11,600**, direct costs increase **€28,000**. Contribution therefore falls **€16,400**, or **6.2%**.

### Enterprise: largest contribution, largest deterioration

- Generates **79.0%** of October direct contribution.
- Contribution falls **€17,300**, more than the total business decline.
- Revenue rises only **€500**, while infrastructure and support costs rise **€17,800**.
- Has the lowest October contribution margin.

**Priority:** investigate infrastructure efficiency, workload mix and customer-success effort. This supports cost-focused engineering—not abandoning the Enterprise customer segment.

The Enterprise **plan** includes six Mid-market customers; plan costs cannot be treated as Enterprise **segment** costs.

### Pro: strongest margin, growing product friction

- Highest October margin: **62.7%**.
- Revenue rises **5.1%**, but direct costs rise **20.5%**.
- Support tickets increase **19 → 28**; rate-limit tickets increase **5 → 13**.

The average **441,889 requests/customer** hides concentration against a **600,000** allowance:

| Customer | October requests | Above included volume |
|---|---:|---:|
| Wonka | 1,020,000 | 420,000 |
| Aperture | 705,000 | 105,000 |
| Vandelay | 690,000 | 90,000 |

Overages are billed at renewal, so current costs can precede associated revenue. However, no overage prices are provided.

**Priority:** targeted rate-limit, capacity and packaging work. Do not infer customer-level profitability or assume every rate-limit ticket results from exceeding the monthly allowance.

### Starter: acquisition growth masks retention weakness

- Contribution rises **€1,900**, but margin falls **2.7 percentage points**.
- Four of nine opening customers churn.
- Reported October revenue includes those customers’ invoices; active-contract exit recurring revenue is only **€21,700**, versus the reported €32,000.
- Average usage falls partly because new, low-usage customers enter and churned customers remain in monthly counts.

**Priority:** retention and onboarding diagnosis before expanding investment. A post-churn margin cannot be calculated without knowing which costs disappear.

## 5. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Do not approve a blanket customer-segment cut.** Address Mid-market revenue losses and SMB Starter churn directly. Obtain segment-level costs before making segment profitability decisions.
2. **Do not approve a broad paid-search spending increase yet.** Separate expansion attribution, collect spend and track matched cohorts through paying-customer conversion. Use controlled incremental tests.
3. **Make Enterprise-plan efficiency the largest engineering workstream**, because it offers the largest observed contribution exposure. Validate specific causes before committing the full quarter.
4. **Give Pro targeted capacity and rate-limit attention**, protecting the strongest-margin plan and investigating renewal overage economics.
5. **Run immediate customer-health actions:** Soylent investigation, Nakatomi and Starter churn reviews, and Wayne renewal preparation.
6. **Replace the headline dashboard** with separate measures for invoices, monthly recurring revenue, exit run rate, bookings, churn and direct contribution.

## 6. UNCERTAINTIES / DATA ISSUES

- **Duplicate invoice:** deduplicate Soylent by transaction ID and reconcile the conflicting source/date records.
- **Identifier mismatch:** joining usage directly on customer ID would omit Wayne and Prestige.
- **Snapshot mismatch:** October plan counts include customers who later churned; they are not closing active counts.
- **Forward bookings versus current contracts:** October expansions total **€216,000 annualised**, equivalent to **€18,000/month** from the following cycle. They are absent from October invoices and current ARR values. Reconcile their effective status before publishing a forward run rate.
- **Billing is not cash collection or accounting revenue recognition.** The normalised figures above are analytical monthly equivalents.
- **Churn timing affects usage:** lower full-month usage for customers leaving mid-month is not necessarily an advance warning signal.
- **No causal proof:** tickets do not establish churn causes; attribution does not establish incremental channel impact.
- **No reliable CAC, payback, LTV or segment profit:** necessary spend, cohort history and cost allocation are missing. Plan contribution also excludes unspecified shared overhead.
- **Only two months are supplied.** Seasonality, durable trends and engineering ROI cannot be established.
- **Date inconsistency:** the environment date precedes the pack’s completed October period. This analysis follows the pack’s explicit instruction that both months are complete; the actual review date should be corrected.
