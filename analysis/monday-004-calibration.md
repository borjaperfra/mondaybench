## What changed since the first battery

The v0.1 calibration —
[`monday-004-calibration-v0.1.md`](monday-004-calibration-v0.1.md), kept
unchanged — returned `REVISE BEFORE FREEZE` on three blocking findings. All
three are now closed, and each was closed by changing MondayBench rather than
the scenario:

1. **The battery did not run under one generation budget.** The token-budget
   audit ([`token-budget-audit.md`](token-budget-audit.md)) established that
   `max_output_tokens` reaches the wire as the legacy `max_tokens` and that
   providers do not apply it consistently to reasoning models — two of seven
   stopped at it exactly, one exceeded it by 84%, four never approached it. It
   therefore cannot equalise compute and MondayBench no longer pretends it does.
   The field is now documented as a **safety ceiling**, set high enough not to
   bind a normal successful generation, and every run records
   `safety_ceiling_tokens`, `ceiling_semantics` and a
   `generation_ceiling_status` of NON_BINDING / BOUND / UNOBSERVABLE. A BOUND
   run cannot qualify. Delivered usage *above* the ceiling is explicitly not a
   rejection: that is a provider's implementation choice, not a candidate's
   doing. See `docs/methodology.md`, "Generation limits are safety ceilings, not
   reasoning budgets".

2. **MIMO v2.5 could not complete the scenario.** It truncated twice at 32,000.
   Under 96,000 it finishes, and so does everything else.

3. **`segment_churn_math` asked for the wrong metric.** Rewritten semantically
   as `segment_retention_quantified` in rubric 0.2: it now asks for retention
   quantified on a stated basis with the populations ranked, and accepts logo
   churn, ARR churn or NRR. It no longer requires a particular phrasing of
   4/18 = 22.2%.

Rubric 0.3 then added the criterion the v0.2 shadow exposed the need for:
`figures_carried_correctly`, 3 points, funded entirely from criteria that were
zero-variance across all six shadow runs. Its scope and the variance evidence
are in [`monday-004-rubric-v0.3.md`](monday-004-rubric-v0.3.md).

**Nothing under monday-001, monday-002 or monday-003 was modified, rescored or
recalculated.** Those scenarios declare no ceiling and keep the 32,000 they were
generated under, so their runs go on qualifying against `models.yaml` unchanged.

## What the ceiling policy actually bought

The change was not cosmetic, and one run proves it.

**GLM 5.2 spent 43,200 completion tokens on the answer that scored 71.0.** Its
pre-policy attempt spent 30,288 and finished, so it had squeaked under the old
32,000 limit by 1,712 tokens. The fresh sample would have been cut off. Under a
32,000 ceiling, whether GLM 5.2 has a valid run on this scenario at all is a coin
flip decided by sampling, not by the model.

**Qwen 3.8 Flash spent 46,434.** Its pre-policy attempt spent 58,956 against a
configured 32,000 — the endpoint did not apply the parameter at all, which is
what the audit documented and what the policy now says plainly: the ceiling is
ours, what a provider does with it is theirs, and delivered usage above it is not
a rejection. Nothing here suggests the model or the endpoint did anything
improper; the parameter simply is not a compute budget.

**MIMO v2.5 finished at 28,597 after truncating twice at exactly 32,000.** The
two rejected attempts are on disk with `finish_reason=length`, and its rejection
notes are in `runs/monday-004/mimo-v2.5/REJECTED-ATTEMPTS.md`, including the
concurrent-retry overwrite disclosed there.

Every pre-policy attempt is preserved, rejected explicitly, and listed above with
its reason. Nothing was deleted and nothing was silently reused.

The cost of the policy is honesty about what is being compared: these seven
models did not spend the same compute, and MondayBench does not claim they did.
Reasoning tokens, completion tokens, TTFA, latency and cost are reported beside
the score, separately from it. Qwen 3.8 Flash and GLM 5.3 Flash tie at 98.5 while
one took 388 seconds and the other 628; DeepSeek V4 Flash reaches 87.0 in 118
seconds. Those are three different products and the table says so.

   concludes wrongly, which is exactly the profile the scenario exists to expose.

## Wording sensitivity

The v0.1 battery found one criterion whose wording, not whose subject, decided
the label: `segment_churn_math` demanded a specific denominator, and a model that
quantified retention correctly on a different basis lost the point. It was
rewritten semantically in 0.2 and the fresh battery shows no successor. The three
criteria most at risk of the same defect were checked directly:

- `segment_retention_quantified` — the three PASSes use three different bases
  (Qwen 3.8: logo churn + ARR churn + NRR; GLM 5.3: logo churn + ARR churn;
  DeepSeek: ARR-weighted churn alone). The criterion accepted all three.
- `headline_vs_recurring_growth` — both accepted normalisations appear among the
  PASSes, and MIMO's PARTIAL is for never computing the raw comparison at all,
  not for computing it differently.
- `figures_carried_correctly` — scoped so that omission is never an error, that
  rounding and a differently-stated basis both pass, and that an error another
  criterion already prices is not charged twice. Two of the seven pass it because
  their only figure errors are priced elsewhere.

One judgment in this battery is close enough that the alternative is stated
rather than buried. Qwen 3.8 Flash's `figures_carried_correctly` is graded
PARTIAL on a single wrong September token aggregate that produces a wrong growth
rate; the FAIL band names "repeated incorrect figures across different parts",
and one aggregate plus its derived rate is arguably not that. Under the stricter
reading it scores 97.0 rather than 98.5, and the ranking does not change.

Condition 6 deserves the most scrutiny, because it is the one that was invisible
in v0.1 and only became visible when a model reached 100.0 under the v0.2 shadow.
It is now measured, and it fires: the two best answers in the battery are best by
a wide margin and neither is perfect. Qwen 3.8 Flash gets every decision and
every trap right and misstates one token aggregate; GLM 5.3 Flash gets every
decision and every trap right and miscounts two ticket totals. 98.5 is the honest
number for both — full credit for analysis that is genuinely excellent, half
credit withheld for arithmetic that carries none of it. Neither failure was
manufactured to create headroom; both were found by checking the responses
against the CSVs and are documented to the digit above.

What a freeze would authorise next is the official three-run battery, which this
report does not launch. That battery will produce the first variance evidence for
this scenario — every model here has `sigma 0.00` because each has exactly one
run, and a scenario whose scores turn out to be unstable across samples is not
publishable however well it calibrates. That is the next gate, not this one.
