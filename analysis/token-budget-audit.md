# Token budget audit — what `max_output_tokens: 32000` actually means

**Read-only.** Nothing in this audit changes a run, an evaluation, a
leaderboard, a qualification rule or a frozen artifact. It exists to establish
the semantics before any methodology change is considered.

Machine-readable companion: `analysis/token-budget-audit.json` — one record per
run, 77 runs across monday-001 … monday-004, regenerated by
`analysis/_token_budget_audit.py`.

---

## 1. What we actually send

`max_output_tokens` is a MondayBench config name. It reaches the wire in exactly
one place, `mondaybench/providers/openai_compatible.py::_payload`:

```python
if generation_config.get("max_output_tokens") is not None:
    payload["max_tokens"] = generation_config["max_output_tokens"]
```

So the outgoing request field is **`max_tokens`**, the legacy OpenAI Chat
Completions parameter. **`max_completion_tokens` is never sent.** No run in this
repository was generated with it. Every one of the seven models is called through
this adapter (`api_style: openai_compatible` for both gateways); the Anthropic
native provider exists for judging only and produced none of these runs.

That distinction matters because in OpenAI's own lineage the two fields do not
mean the same thing — `max_tokens` bounds the completion, and
`max_completion_tokens` was introduced precisely to bound reasoning *plus*
visible output for reasoning models. But Helmcode and NaN are third-party
gateways fronting open models, not OpenAI, so **OpenAI's documented semantics are
not authoritative for them** and are not used as evidence below. Everything in
§3 is established from observed behaviour in this repository.

## 2. What we get back

`provider_metadata.raw_usage` stores the provider's usage object verbatim. Two
shapes appear:

```
completion_tokens, prompt_tokens, total_tokens,
completion_tokens_details: { reasoning_tokens, audio_tokens, image_tokens, text_tokens }
```

The harness records `output_tokens = usage.completion_tokens` and
`reasoning_tokens = completion_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens`.

**Is `reasoning_tokens` inside `completion_tokens` or beside it?** Answered
empirically, not by field name. If it is inside, then
`completion_tokens − reasoning_tokens` is the visible answer, and dividing the
answer's character count by it should land near the usual 3–5 characters per
token. If it is beside, then `completion_tokens` alone should give that ratio.

| provider/model | runs | reasoning reported | median chars / derived answer token | median chars / completion token |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| helmcode/deepseek-v4-flash | 11 | yes | **4.19** | 0.38 |
| helmcode/gemma4 | 10 | no (always 0) | 3.96 | 3.96 |
| helmcode/glm5.2 | 10 | yes | **3.98** | 1.49 |
| helmcode/qwen3.6 | 10 | no (always 0) | 3.60 | 3.60 |
| nan/glm5.3-flash | 10 | yes | **3.99** | 0.68 |
| nan/mimo-v2.5 | 11 | no (always 0) | 0.73 | 0.73 |
| nan/qwen3.8-flash | 15 | yes | **3.76** | 0.66 |

For every model that reports reasoning, the derived-answer ratio lands in the
normal range and the completion ratio is absurd. **`reasoning_tokens` is a subset
of `completion_tokens`** on both gateways. Qwen 3.8 Flash confirms it from both
sides: its four runs that report zero reasoning give 3.45–3.83 chars per
completion token, and its eleven runs that report reasoning give 3.24–4.48 chars
per *derived answer* token. Same model, same ratio, whichever way it is served.

**MIMO v2.5 is the exception and is exceptional in a specific way.** It reports
`reasoning_tokens: 0` on all eleven runs, yet its chars-per-completion-token runs
at 0.29–0.99 on monday-002 … monday-004 (and 2.29–2.35 even on monday-001).
Something is consuming its completion tokens that is not in the visible answer
and is not reported as reasoning. `models.yaml` already documents the cause:

> Streams a thinking phase but reports `reasoning_tokens=0`; delivery is
> established from the pre-answer stream instead of the counter.

So MIMO's reasoning **is** inside `completion_tokens` and simply is not broken
out. Its `answer_tokens_derived` column in the JSON is therefore wrong by
construction and is not used anywhere in this audit.

## 3. What the ceiling bounds, per provider and model

Only two kinds of run carry evidence: one that stopped at exactly the ceiling
(shows what the ceiling bounds) and one that went past it (shows it was not
applied). Everything else is silent.

| provider/model | runs | max completion seen | stopped at ceiling | exceeded | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| helmcode/deepseek-v4-flash | 11 | 32,000 | 1 | 0 | **bounds reasoning + answer** |
| nan/mimo-v2.5 | 11 | 32,000 | 2 | 0 | **bounds reasoning + answer** |
| nan/qwen3.8-flash | 15 | 58,956 | 0 | **5** | **not applied to completion** |
| helmcode/gemma4 | 10 | 1,546 | 0 | 0 | UNKNOWN — never approached it |
| helmcode/glm5.2 | 10 | 30,288 | 0 | 0 | UNKNOWN — never reached it |
| helmcode/qwen3.6 | 10 | 4,336 | 0 | 0 | UNKNOWN — never approached it |
| nan/glm5.3-flash | 10 | 22,838 | 0 | 0 | UNKNOWN — never reached it |

The two "bounds reasoning + answer" verdicts are strong:

```
monday-004  deepseek-v4-flash  run-001  completion 32,000 = ceiling
                                        reasoning 30,739, visible answer 1,261 tokens
                                        finish_reason=length
monday-004  mimo-v2.5          run-001  completion 32,000 = ceiling, answer 9,182 chars
monday-004  mimo-v2.5          run-002  completion 32,000 = ceiling, answer 16,655 chars
```

In all three the visible answer is a small fraction of 32,000 and generation
still stopped, so the ceiling was counting the reasoning phase against the
budget. That is semantics **A**.

The qwen3.8-flash verdict is equally strong in the other direction:

| scenario | run | configured | completion | reasoning | derived answer | over by | finish |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| monday-002 | 001 | 32,000 | 44,910 | 42,808 | 2,102 | +12,910 | stop |
| monday-002 | 003 | 32,000 | 33,714 | 30,342 | 3,372 | +1,714 | stop |
| monday-003 | 002 | 32,000 | 49,968 | 46,603 | 3,365 | +17,968 | stop |
| monday-003 | 007 | 32,000 | 36,111 | 33,817 | 2,294 | +4,111 | stop |
| monday-004 | 001 | 32,000 | 58,956 | 48,630 | 10,326 | +26,956 | stop |

Every one finished cleanly. This is **not a new discovery**: `models.yaml` has
recorded it since 2026-08-28, before monday-002 and monday-003 were published.

> On NaN the cap is advisory: qwen3.8-flash returned 35 completion tokens against
> `max_tokens=1`, and 10,464 against 4,000. Expect `finish_reason=length` to be
> unreliable there — the runner records whatever the provider reports.

The `max_tokens=1` probe is decisive on its own: a provider that returns 35
tokens against a ceiling of 1 is not enforcing the field at all.

**What we cannot establish:** whether NaN applies the ceiling to the *visible
answer* (semantics B) or ignores it entirely (semantics C). Qwen 3.8 Flash's
largest visible answer in 15 runs is 10,326 tokens, less than a third of the
ceiling, so no run ever tested the visible-answer bound. The `max_tokens=1`
probe returned 35 completion tokens of which 29 were reasoning — 6 visible,
which is also above 1. That leans towards "not enforced", but one probe against
a degenerate value is not enough to call it. **Marked UNKNOWN.**

## 4. Answer to the question

> Is `max_output_tokens=32000` intended to mean (A) reasoning + answer combined,
> (B) final visible answer only, (C) provider-specific / unsupported, or (D)
> something else?

**Intended: A.** The project's own record establishes it. `models.yaml`
documents the 16,000 → 32,000 raise:

> deepseek-v4-flash hit the same wall glm5.3-flash hit at 4,000: two consecutive
> attempts produced 69,501 and 70,207 characters of reasoning and no answer at
> all […] Raising it for one model would have been per-model tuning to rescue
> that model, so it was raised for all seven and the whole battery regenerated.

A budget raised because a model spent all of it reasoning is a budget that is
understood to bound reasoning. `qualify_run` rejecting `finish_reason=length`
encodes the same intent.

**Observed: C.** The single configured value is honoured as A by
helmcode/deepseek-v4-flash and nan/mimo-v2.5, is not applied to the completion
total by nan/qwen3.8-flash, and is untested for the remaining four
provider/model pairs. Whether NaN's non-enforcement is B or a true C is UNKNOWN
on repository evidence.

## 5. Historical impact of the hypothetical rule

> **Hypothetical, NOT APPLIED:** delivered completion usage must not exceed
> configured `max_output_tokens`.

### Affected runs

Five runs across the whole repository, all `qwen3.8-flash`, all `finish_reason=stop`.
No other model exceeds its ceiling in any scenario.

| scenario | run | published as | score | over by |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| monday-002 | run-001 | **official** | 93.0 | +12,910 |
| monday-002 | run-003 | **official** | 100.0 | +1,714 |
| monday-003 | run-002 | **official** | 100.0 | +17,968 |
| monday-003 | run-007 | **official** | 88.5 | +4,111 |
| monday-004 | run-001 | calibration draft | 97.5 | +26,956 |

### Affected models

One: Qwen 3.8 Flash. monday-001 is untouched — its largest completion is 10,212
tokens.

### Affected published scores

| scenario | published | official runs | would survive | surviving mean |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| monday-002 | Qwen 3.8 Flash **94.8**, rank 1 | 001 (93.0), 003 (100.0), 004 (91.5) | 004 only | 91.5 |
| monday-003 | Qwen 3.8 Flash **94.5**, rank 2 | 001 (95.0), 002 (100.0), 007 (88.5) | 001 only | 95.0 |

### Could rankings change?

**monday-002 — yes, the top of the table changes.** Qwen 3.8 Flash is rank 1 at
94.8 and GLM 5.2 is rank 2 at 91.2. Both of Qwen's over-budget runs are removed,
including its 100.0. On the single surviving run it scores 91.5 — still ahead of
91.2, by 0.3 points. But one run is not a result: the publication gate requires
three, so the honest outcome is that Qwen 3.8 Flash leaves the battery and
**GLM 5.2 becomes rank 1**.

**monday-003 — yes, and in Qwen's favour.** It is rank 2 at 94.5 behind
DeepSeek's 95.0. The two removed runs are its 100.0 and its 88.5 — its best and
its worst. The survivor scores 95.0, which would **tie DeepSeek exactly**. Again
one run cannot be published, so the practical outcome is that Qwen 3.8 Flash
leaves the battery and DeepSeek's rank 1 stands unchallenged.

Note the asymmetry: the rule removes Qwen's best run in both scenarios, and in
monday-003 removes its worst as well. It is not a uniformly punitive rule and it
is not a uniformly favourable one.

### Are there enough replacement runs?

**No, in neither scenario.**

| scenario | runs on disk | already excluded | over budget | left |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| monday-002 | 001, 002, 003, 004 | 002 (REASONING_NOT_DELIVERED) | 001, 003 | **1** |
| monday-003 | 001 … 007 | 003, 004, 005, 006 (REASONING_NOT_DELIVERED) | 002, 007 | **1** |

Both fall from three qualifying runs to one. `publish` would refuse each
leaderboard outright rather than rank six models beside one measured on a single
run. Restoring either scenario would require regenerating Qwen 3.8 Flash — and
under the hypothetical rule there is no guarantee a regenerated run would qualify,
because the provider does not enforce the ceiling that the rule tests.

### Was the historical comparison actually unfair?

**In monday-001, monday-002 and monday-003: no. It was inconsistent in
enforcement but not unfair in effect.**

The decisive fact is that **no run in those three scenarios was truncated**:

| scenario | truncated runs | 2nd-highest completion (i.e. the most any other model used) |
|---|---|---:|
| monday-001 | none | 6,117 |
| monday-002 | none | 20,622 |
| monday-003 | none | 24,321 |
| monday-004 | **3** | 32,000 (both truncated) |

In the published scenarios the ceiling bound nobody. Every other model stopped
because it had finished, not because it ran out. Qwen 3.8 Flash used more
reasoning than the others, but that headroom was available to all seven and none
of them was denied it. The advantage it gained is the ordinary advantage of a
model that thinks longer — which is what the benchmark is measuring — and the
cost of that thinking shows up honestly in the published TTFA and latency
columns.

**In monday-004: yes, genuinely unfair.** Three runs across two models were cut
off at 32,000 while Qwen 3.8 Flash was served 58,956. There the ceiling
determined who could finish, and it was applied to some competitors and not to
the winner. That is a different situation from the published three, and it is
the reason monday-004 is not ready for an official battery.

### One caveat on "not unfair"

The finding above is about *effect*, not about the record being clean. Two things
remain true and should be stated wherever the published results are cited:

1. monday-002 and monday-003 were not run under a uniformly enforced budget.
2. `qualify_run` cannot detect this: it compares the *configured* value recorded
   in the run against `models.yaml` and never checks *delivered* usage against
   it. Any future scenario has the same blind spot.

## 6. The monday-004 truncations (Part B)

Three runs were cut off, across two models. All three are preserved and none
contributes to any statistic.

| | deepseek-v4-flash 001 | mimo-v2.5 001 | mimo-v2.5 002 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| finish_reason | `length` | `length` | `length` |
| completion tokens | 32,000 | 32,000 | 32,000 |
| configured ceiling | 32,000 | 32,000 | 32,000 |
| reasoning tokens reported | 30,739 | 0 | 0 |
| answer characters | 4,356 | 9,182 | 16,655 |
| answer tokens (derived / est.) | 1,261 | ~2,300 | ~4,160 |
| TTFT | 2.2s | 2.6s | 2.0s |
| TTFA (first answer token) | 166.0s | 388.7s | 338.1s |
| pre-answer thinking | **163.9s** | **386.1s** | **336.1s** |
| answer empty or partial? | partial | partial | partial |

### Was 32,000 actually reached?

Yes, exactly, in all three. `completion_tokens` equals the configured ceiling to
the token. This is not a provider-side limit that happens to sit nearby — it is
our number.

### Did truncation happen during reasoning or during the answer?

**During the answer, in all three** — but only because reasoning had already
spent the budget.

- DeepSeek: 30,739 of the 32,000 (96%) went to reasoning. The model then emitted
  1,261 tokens of answer and stopped mid-sentence, in the middle of
  "Trial-to-paid".
- MIMO run-001: 386 seconds elapsed between the first token and the first
  *answer* token, and the answer that follows is ~2,300 tokens. Roughly 29,700
  completion tokens went to a thinking phase the provider does not report. It
  stops mid-row inside the per-customer economics table.
- MIMO run-002: 336 seconds of thinking, ~4,160 tokens of answer, stops
  mid-sentence in the recommendations.

None of the three produced an empty answer. All three produced good partial work
— DeepSeek's fragment already identifies the duplicate and refuses CAC payback;
MIMO's already builds a correct MRR bridge and resolves the Wayne alias.

### Is this caused by our limit or by the provider?

**By our configured limit.** Both providers applied `max_tokens=32000` to these
two models faithfully, counting the reasoning phase against it — semantics A,
consistent with §3. Nothing provider-side intervened: raise the number and these
runs would complete.

The MIMO case additionally confirms §2's finding. `reasoning_tokens: 0` with a
386-second gap between TTFT and TTFA is a reporting gap, not an absence of
reasoning, exactly as `models.yaml` records. Its thinking is inside
`completion_tokens` and invisible in `completion_tokens_details`.

### Why this is not the same problem as §5

The historical over-budget runs and these truncations are the two halves of one
inconsistency, but only one of them costs anything:

- In monday-001 … monday-003 nobody was truncated, so Qwen 3.8 Flash's extra
  reasoning cost no competitor anything.
- In monday-004 the ceiling decides who finishes. DeepSeek and MIMO were stopped
  at 32,000; Qwen 3.8 Flash, on the same configured 32,000, was served 58,956 and
  scored 97.5. That is one experiment with two different rules.

monday-004 is the first scenario demanding enough to reach the ceiling at all,
which is why the inconsistency only becomes material now.
