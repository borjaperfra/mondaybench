## Penalties

- **GLM 5.2** PROHIBITED_ACTION (−7) — Recommends defunding part of the SMB
  segment on economics that belong to the Starter plan; the two are not the same
  population. Its own segment walk shows SMB net ARR up €9.6k in October and
  Mid-market down €174k. The pack contains no segment-level economics and the
  response does not say so.
- **Qwen 3.6** PROHIBITED_ACTION (−7) — Recommends defunding the SMB segment on
  a four-event churn count and on the Starter plan's margin, when the four
  churns cost less ARR than one Mid-market logo and SMB is 6.6% of the book.
  This is the scenario's canonical wrong answer.
- **Qwen 3.6** UNSUPPORTED_CAUSALITY (−5) — "The churn rate in SMB is high,
  indicating that the product is not delivering enough value to retain these
  customers." No file records why any customer left, and the claim carries the
  defunding recommendation.

Two of six runs drew a penalty; four drew none. That is the intended shape:
penalties price the two models that acted on the traps, not the models that
merely scored less.

**What this says, model by model.**

- **Gemma 4 and Qwen 3.6 fail at data cleaning, and everything downstream
  follows.** Both score 0% there: neither found the duplicated invoice and
  neither found the annual prepayment, so both reported a revenue picture that
  was wrong before any judgement was applied. Gemma 4 then told leadership the
  business was in "high-growth". Notably, both still score 65% on business
  judgment and 85%/50% on uncertainty — they know the shape of a good brief.
  What they cannot do is the arithmetic underneath it, and the format hides
  that: Gemma 4's answer reads like a competent memo and earns 42.
- **DeepSeek V4 Flash fails at table joining (50%) while cleaning perfectly
  (100%).** It found the duplicate and the prepayment and normalised both, and
  then never joined billing to usage at account level, so Wayne Enterprises is
  absent from the response and support is never compared across plans. Its one
  interpretive error is the same shape: "All plans are below included request
  volumes" is read off the plan averages, when two Enterprise and three Pro
  customers are above. It is an aggregate-blindness failure, which is the thing
  the scenario is named after.
- **The top three differ almost entirely on judgment, not on arithmetic.** All
  three clean and normalise perfectly. GLM 5.2 loses 7 points to a
  recommendation, not a calculation. GLM 5.3 Flash loses on two judgement calls.
  Qwen 3.8 Flash loses 2.5 points, on a criterion that is arguably wrong (below).
- **Uncertainty is the easiest dimension in the scenario.** Every model refused
  to compute CAC payback, and every model produced a usable uncertainties
  section. That is a real result — this cohort has learned to say "I cannot
  compute this" — and it means D12 discriminates nothing.

## Three blocking findings

### 1. The battery did not run under one generation budget

`max_output_tokens: 32000` was requested for every model and accepted by every
provider — `unsupported_parameters` is empty in all seven runs. It was then
honoured for some and not others:

| model | configured | delivered | outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| MIMO v2.5 | 32,000 | 32,000 ×3 | cut off, no qualifying run |
| DeepSeek V4 Flash | 32,000 | 32,000 | cut off on attempt 1 |
| GLM 5.2 | 32,000 | 30,288 | fitted, barely |
| **Qwen 3.8 Flash** | 32,000 | **58,956** | **ceiling not applied** |

The highest-scoring run in the battery was allowed 84% more output than the two
runs that were truncated. That is not a comparability footnote; it is the
battery not being one experiment.

**This is not confined to monday-004.** The same model exceeded the same ceiling
in the published batteries:

```
monday-002  qwen3.8-flash  run-001  44,910 / 32,000
monday-002  qwen3.8-flash  run-003  33,714 / 32,000
monday-003  qwen3.8-flash  run-002  49,968 / 32,000
monday-003  qwen3.8-flash  run-007  36,111 / 32,000
monday-004  qwen3.8-flash  run-001  58,956 / 32,000
```

`qualify_run` compares the *configured* budget recorded in the run against
`models.yaml` and never checks the *delivered* token count against it, so none
of these was caught. No other model exceeds its ceiling in any scenario.

I have **not** added the gate. Doing so would retroactively disqualify four
official runs in two frozen, published scenarios, and that is a publication
decision rather than a calibration one. It is the first thing to settle.

### 2. MIMO v2.5 cannot complete the scenario

Three attempts, all cut off at 32,000 output tokens with zero reasoning tokens
reported. The answers are good where they get to — run-001 finds the duplicate,
builds a correct MRR bridge and resolves the Wayne alias — and both stop before
the recommendations. `models.yaml` already records that this provider does not
count MIMO's reasoning, so the most likely reading is that it reasons inside the
answer envelope and runs out.

The budget cannot be raised for monday-004 alone: `max_output_tokens` is set per
model in `models.yaml` and shared with the three frozen scenarios, whose
official runs would stop qualifying. The fix is either an additive
higher-budget model entry, or a shorter required response format, or both.

### 3. `segment_churn_math` asks for the wrong metric

**Every one of the six qualifying runs scored PARTIAL on it. Zero passed.** The
criterion asks for logo churn by segment — 4 of 18, 1 of 10, 0 of 8. What the
four strong models actually computed was ARR-weighted churn, net ARR movement,
or per-segment NRR, all correct and all more useful. They lose 2.5 points each
for choosing the better metric.

A criterion that nobody passes is 2.5 points of constant mass and no signal. It
is also the only thing keeping the top run off 100.0, which means the headline
result of this calibration is currently decided by a rubric defect.

## Scenario quality checks (§18 of the brief)

1. **No single table contains a decision.** Confirmed — and confirmed against a
   known partial leak: `plans.csv` on a recurring basis lets a model reach "flat"
   without the prepayment. Qwen 3.6 did exactly that and still lost 11 of the 20
   revenue points, because the criteria price identifying the annual invoice, not
   arriving at flat.
2. **Several criteria need cross-table reasoning.** `customer_id_alias_resolved`,
   `support_normalized`, `cross_table_customer_signal` and `product_focus_supported`
   all do, and they separate the field: 15.0 / 13.5 / 13.0 / 5.5 / 4.0 / 3.5 out
   of 15 on that dimension.
3. **A calculation requires cleaning first.** The duplicate. Four models removed
   it; two never saw it.
4. **A trend reverses after normalisation.** +12.0% → +1.6% recurring, or −4.2%
   including services. Qwen 3.8 Flash produced both.
5. **A requested conclusion is unsupportable.** CAC payback — refused by 6 of 6.
6. **Weak models make attractive-but-wrong recommendations.** Qwen 3.6
   recommended cutting SMB; Gemma 4 recommended cutting Mid-market and reported
   12% growth. Both produced confident, well-formatted briefs.
7. **Prose summarisation earns nothing.** Gemma 4 is the proof: a clean, correct
   memo structure, and 42.0.

## Rubric changes to make before a v0.2 battery

1. **Rewrite `segment_churn_math`** to accept any correctly labelled churn rate —
   logo, ARR-weighted or NRR — and to require only that the framing (segment vs
   plan) is stated and consistent. As written it penalises the better answer.
2. **Split `segment_recommendation` from the `prohibited_business_action`
   penalty, or sharpen the penalty.** GLM 5.2 lost 3 points on the criterion and
   7 on the penalty for one decision, and its version of that decision ("cut SMB
   CS headcount and outbound, keep the inbound funnel") is materially more
   defensible than Qwen 3.6's flat "yes, reduce investment", yet both cost 7.
   The penalty should name the bar precisely: recommending a reduction *without
   engaging the ARR at stake or naming the missing segment economics*.
3. **Make dimension D discriminate.** `contribution_calculated`,
   `margin_calculated_or_correctly_compared` and `enterprise_revenue_not_margin`
   are 6/6, 6/6 and 6/6 — 14 points that every model earns, including the two
   that could not read the revenue file. `plans.csv` hands over a one-line
   subtraction. Give infra cost per million tokens and support cost per ticket
   instead, and make the model build plan economics from `usage.csv` and
   `support.csv`. That also gives `support_normalized` something harder to do.
4. **Widen `support_normalized`** to name active users, requests and revenue as
   acceptable denominators alongside customers — Qwen 3.8 Flash and GLM 5.2 both
   normalised sensibly on denominators the criterion does not list.
5. **State in `customer_id_alias_resolved`** that a response which never attempts
   an account-level join scores FAIL rather than PARTIAL. Three runs sat in that
   gap and the criterion text does not cover it.
6. **Consider dropping or repricing `cac_payback_unavailable`** (3 points, 6/6)
   and `uncertainty_discipline` (2 points, 6/6). This cohort has learned to
   flag missing data; those 5 points no longer measure anything.

## Ambiguities found while judging

- **Two normalisations of D1 disagree and both are right.** Subscriptions only
  gives +1.6%; subscriptions plus one-off services gives −4.2%, because
  September carried €51,000 of professional services against October's €5,700.
  The rubric's first draft accepted only the first. Fixed during calibration —
  `headline_vs_recurring_growth` and the answer key now name both, and a test
  asserts both refute the headline.
- **`product_focus_supported` and the Enterprise answer.** Two models chose
  Enterprise. GLM 5.3's case (falling margin, reliability ticket theme,
  batch-workload demand) is much stronger than DeepSeek's (majority of revenue,
  falling margin), and both scored PARTIAL. The criterion currently cannot tell
  a good non-canonical answer from a weak one.
- **Wayne is the second-largest account, not the third.** Corrected in the
  design notes and the answer key during calibration.
- **Latency is not measurable in this battery** — three runs were generated
  concurrently against one provider after a fourth hung for thirty minutes.

## RECOMMENDATION: REVISE BEFORE FREEZE

The scenario itself is in good shape. It discriminates across a 59-point spread
with 19 of 25 criteria separating models, its traps fire on the models that
deserve them, its penalties do not fire on the models that do not, and the
top score survives a line-by-line audit. The design goal — telling you *why* a
model failed — works: Gemma 4 and Qwen 3.6 fail at data cleaning, DeepSeek fails
at joining, and the top three differ only on judgement.

It cannot be frozen as it stands, for three reasons that have nothing to do with
how good the scenario is:

1. Seven models were asked to answer under one budget and were not given one.
   The winner had 84% more output than the two models that were cut off, and the
   same defect is present in two already-published batteries.
2. One of the seven models has no qualifying run at all.
3. The criterion that decides whether the top run scores 97.5 or 100.0 is one
   that no model passed, because it asks for a cruder metric than any of them
   chose to compute.

Settle the budget question first — it is the one that reaches back into
monday-002 and monday-003 — then apply the six rubric changes above and run a
v0.2 calibration before any official three-run battery.
